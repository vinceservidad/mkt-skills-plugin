# Editing checklist

## 4Cs (Bly)

- Clear: one idea per sentence; no jargon the reader doesn't use; short paragraphs.
- Concise: cut "very", "really", "in order to", hedges ("we believe"), and repeated points.
- Compelling: lead with the reader's outcome; "you" over "we"; active verbs.
- Credible: a number, a name or role, a date; proof next to the claim.

## Anti-AI check (applies to everything written)

Fix every one of these before saving or delivering copy:

1. "It's not X, it's Y": just say Y.
2. Question hooks and rhetorical setups ("What if I told you", "Think about it:", "Plot twist:"): lead with the answer, the result or the number.
3. Buzzwords (game-changer, unlock, leverage, elevate, delve, robust, streamline, supercharge, harness, transformative): use plain words. Keep a word when it's the exact platform term.
4. "What do you think?" endings, mic-drop closing lines, recap endings ("Overall", "In conclusion"): end on the point.
5. Bullets always in threes: use as many as the point needs.
6. Vague claims: use real figures from my notes, the data or a named source. If there are none, cut the claim or mark the gap.
7. Colon reveals ("The best part: it learns."): write a plain sentence. Use colons only for lists, labels and quotes.
8. Trailing "-ing" clauses that claim meaning (highlighting, showcasing, underscoring, reflecting): cut them or state the fact.
9. Weasel attribution ("experts agree", "studies show"): name the source or cut the claim. Never invent a source.
10. Synonym cycling (agent, then assistant, then tool for the same thing): repeat the clear word. For metrics, use the platform's name every time.
11. Negative listing ("Not a X. Not a Y. A Z."): state Z.
12. Dramatic fragments and robotic rhythm ("That's it. That's the whole thing."): write full sentences and vary their length.
13. Em dashes: none in short text (messages, posts, ad copy), at most 2 in long text.
14. Formatting slop: no emoji in headers, no mid-sentence bold.
15. Portability test: if a line could fit any other person, client or product unchanged, make it specific or cut it.

### When editing the user's own draft

Keep the user's voice (their hedges, bluntness, asides and long spoken sentences), make the smallest edit that fixes the problem, and show the draft, then a short "What changed" list.

## Techniques

- Slippery slide (Sugarman): every sentence earns the next; the first sentence is short.
- Bucket brigades: short transitions sparingly, without colon reveals or rhetorical setups.
- Damaging admission: an honest limitation raises credibility. Keep the real ones.
- Future pacing: let the reader picture the after-state, without promising beyond the offer.

## Claims

| Copy says | Ask | Edit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "The best…" | proof? | replace with a specific, provable difference, or flag it |
| "Guaranteed results" | is it in offer.md? | use the exact guarantee terms, or flag it |
| "Thousands of happy customers" | the real number? | use the sourced number, or flag *needs proof* |
