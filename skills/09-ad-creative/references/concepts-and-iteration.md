# Concepts, native formats, and iteration

## Concept, variation, iteration, adaptation

- **Concept:** one way into the message: an angle at an awareness level, carried by a hook, a story and an offer. Two concepts differ in angle or story, not in wording.
- **Variation:** the same concept with exactly one element changed (hook, visual, proof, CTA). Name the element.
- **Iteration:** the next round, designed from what the last round showed.
- **Adaptation:** the same ad resized or recropped for a placement. Not a test.

## Hook–Story–Offer

- **Hook:** the first frame, first line and on-screen text. See the `hooks` skill for the library.
- **Story:** the beats that make the viewer care: problem in their words → what they tried → the mechanism or turn → proof → what life looks like. Tie each beat to a message, story or proof item.
- **Offer:** one call to action to a real offer, and the page it lands on. The offer's terms are exactly those in `offer.md`.

## Native formats

A native ad looks like the content around it: a feed post, a creator video, a document a peer would share, an article on a publisher site. Native is about form, not disguise: paid content stays labelled as an ad where law or platform policy requires it (check current rules; *to confirm*).

| Format | Carries well | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Static image | One idea, one proof point | Too much text; claims without proof |
| Carousel or document | A process, a sample, a before/after sequence | Each card must earn the next |
| Short vertical video | A story, a demonstration, a founder or customer talking | Hook in frame one; captions for sound-off |
| UGC-style video | Peer voice, unboxing, day-in-the-life | Must be a real user's real experience; see `ugc-brief` |
| Native placement / advertorial | Long-form explanation for problem- and solution-aware readers | Disclosure; claims must hold up in long form |

Format fit is a hypothesis. Specs and placements: platform documentation with the date checked, or *to confirm*.

## Reading past results

1. Separate sources: platform-reported metrics (impressions, frequency, clicks, platform leads) and business outcomes from the CRM or orders. Different systems, different definitions; never add them together.
2. Compute cost per business outcome per concept, showing the formula (spend ÷ qualified outcomes) and the period.
3. Check the comparison is clean: same audience, same destination, same period, same budget logic. If concepts differ in destination or format as well as angle, the comparison is confounded: say what can and can't be concluded.
4. Small samples: with only a handful of outcomes per concept, say the difference may be noise and name the minimum sample or time to judge.
5. Fatigue: falling CTR while frequency rises is a pattern consistent with fatigue. It is a hypothesis; the audience saturating, seasonality or the destination are other explanations.
6. Decide: keep, iterate, refresh or retire, with the evidence. High clicks with unqualified outcomes is not a winner.

## What this skill does not decide

Budget, bids, audiences, scaling and live ad changes need the owner's authorization and belong to `19-scale-paid` (readiness, economics, marginal evidence and guardrails) and the channel owner. Recommend creative only.
