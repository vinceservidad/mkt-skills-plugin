# Trust stack: signal types, take-down rules, placement

Social proof and authority (Cialdini) are **principles** about how people decide under uncertainty. A trust signal applies them only when it is true; a fake or unsupported one is a dark pattern and a legal and reputational risk. Placement rules below are **heuristics** to test.

## Signal types

| Signal | Use when | Never |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reviews | Verbatim, with source, date and rating; link to the review platform where possible | Edit a review, combine two into one, or invent a "Happy customer" quote |
| Testimonials | A named or role-described customer who consented; specific before and after | Use a support ticket or private message without consent |
| Real people | Founder, team, the accountant or roaster the buyer will deal with | Stock photos presented as staff or customers |
| Specific numbers | From a named source with period ("94% of client-months in 2025, from our close log, self-reported") | Round up, drop the period, or say "thousands" without a count |
| Client logos | Clients who agreed to be shown | Logos of prospects, past trials or brands you merely sell |
| Guarantees | Owner-approved wording, with the conditions | A "100% satisfaction" badge with no policy behind it |
| Security and payment cues | What is true of the checkout | Certification or compliance badges the business does not hold, or a provider's badge used against its rules (check the provider's own documentation) |
| Press, awards | A real mention with date and link | "As seen in" without the mention |

## Take-down rules

Take down (or replace with a true version) any element that is:
- **unsupported:** a number, rating or claim with no source;
- **unapproved:** a guarantee, policy or promise the owner has not approved, or that depends on something not built;
- **untraceable:** a testimonial not found in any review export or permitted source, even if it appears in the current page copy;
- **inconsistent:** contradicted elsewhere on the site or by the checkout (a free-shipping badge that applies only to some orders).

Removing a false signal is a fix, not a test; still get the owner's approval before changing the live page.

## Fears by decision point

| Point | Typical fear | Signal that answers it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Above the fold | Is this for someone like me? Is it real? | A peer review from the target buyer; the real people behind it |
| Near the price | Is it worth it? What if it isn't? | Specific results; the approved guarantee |
| At the form or button | What happens next? Will I be spammed or locked in? | What happens after; cancellation and privacy terms that exist |
| Checkout | Is my payment safe? Can I cancel? | True security cues; renewal and cancel terms |

Use the business's own evidenced fears from Beliefs, objections and drivers; the table is only a starting map.

## Strength

Strong: specific, third-party, dated, permitted, about the exact fear. Weak: vague, self-reported, one source, or contradicted by other evidence. Do not use contradicted proof as reassurance where it would mislead (a timing claim when late deliveries are the largest complaint).

## Permission and privacy

Mark every quote, name, photo and logo needing permission. Public reviews may still need the reviewer's permission for use in ads or prominent placements; follow the business's policy and mark *to check*. Remove identifying details of private people.

## Measuring

Primary: orders or qualified leads from the page. Guardrails: refunds, complaints, support questions about a claim. A trust change is tested like any other; removing a false claim is done regardless of the result.
