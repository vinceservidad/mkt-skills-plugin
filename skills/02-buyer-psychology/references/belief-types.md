# Belief and objection types

## Brunson's three false beliefs (Expert Secrets)

| Type | The buyer believes… | Example | Usually flipped by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vehicle | this kind of solution can't get me the result | "Bookkeepers can't tell me which clients to fire; I need a CFO" | a demonstration on their own case; reframing the category |
| Internal | I can't make it work, or I'll lose something | "If I outsource, I lose control" | a clear process, control retained, small first step |
| External | something outside my control will stop it | "My clients pay late whatever I do" | a story of someone in the same situation; a mechanism that handles it |

## Practical objection types

| Type | Sounds like | What it often really is | Handled in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Price or value | "too expensive", "can't justify it" | value not shown, wrong segment, or a missing anchor | 07-offer (value equation, pricing, anchoring) |
| Trust | "they all promise the same", "how do I know?" | no proof specific to them | 08-message (proof stack); 16-referral (testimonials) |
| Risk | "what if it doesn't work / the first month is late?" | fear of loss | 07-offer (guarantee, risk reversal) |
| Fit | "not for someone my size", "too much for me" | a real mismatch, so disqualify or package differently | 02-customer (ICP disqualifiers); 07-offer (value ladder) |
| Timing | "not ready", "maybe next quarter" | no trigger, or another priority | 03-awareness (stage); 15-retention (nurture) |
| Category | "you look like X, I need Y" | positioning | 06-positioning |

## Persuasion drivers and the evidence that justifies them

| Driver | Use when the evidence shows… | Do not use when… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social proof (Cialdini) | peers' choices sway them (referrals close better; they ask "who else uses you?") | no real peer results exist yet |
| Authority (Cialdini) | they ask about expertise or specialisation | the claim cannot be backed |
| Reciprocity (Cialdini) | a useful free asset leads to calls or trials | the free thing has no standalone value |
| Commitment and consistency (Cialdini) | small first steps lead to bigger ones | it would trap them into something they did not choose |
| Liking (Cialdini) | the relationship is cited in reviews | — |
| Scarcity and urgency (Cialdini) | a real capacity limit or deadline exists | it would be invented; never fabricate |
| Loss aversion (Kahneman) | they talk about avoiding a loss (payroll scare, wasted money) | it would exaggerate fear beyond the evidence |
| Anchoring (Kahneman, Ariely) | they compare against a higher-priced alternative | no honest comparison exists |
| Motivation–ability–prompt (Fogg) | the blocker is effort (onboarding, setup) or a missing prompt | the blocker is a belief |
| Life-Force 8 (Whitman) | a core desire is visible in their words (safety, care for loved ones, status) | it would be imposed on them |

## Ranking

- Score each objection: frequency (count in evidence) × deal impact (did it end deals, or was it raised and overcome?).
- "Price" is the most common stated objection and often a proxy. Check whether lost-on-price buyers were outside the ICP, or saw no value proof.
