# Positioning Classics (Reeves, Ries & Trout)

Apply these checks alongside April Dunford's *Obviously Awesome* order once competitive alternatives, unique attributes, and customer value are grounded in evidence.

## 1. 22 Immutable Laws of Marketing (Ries & Trout)

Check each draft position against the core strategic filters from Al Ries and Jack Trout:

- **Law of Leadership / Law of the Category:** if evidence shows you cannot win head-on in an existing category, define a sharper subsegment or a new category (handed to `06-category-design`) where you can be first.
- **Law of the Mind:** better evidence-backed positioning earlier in the buyer's memory beats a slightly "better" checklist feature later. Pick one clear concept buyers can remember.
- **Law of Focus:** own one concrete word or attribute in the buyer's mind instead of bundling five unrelated benefits.
- **Law of Sacrifice:** name clearly who the position is not for and what services, buyer sizes, or use cases you give up to keep the main claim sharp.
- **Law of the Opposite:** if a strong dominant leader owns one trait (established, enterprise, broad), consider positioning cleanly as the opposite when buyer switching evidence supports it (fast, focused, built for one job), without inventing competitor weaknesses.
- **Law of Line Extension:** warn if the business tries to sell incompatible offers or price tiers under the exact same positioning frame.

## 2. Three-Part USP Test (Rosser Reeves)

From Rosser Reeves (*Reality in Advertising*), validate any candidate USP in `## Differentiation`:

1. **Specific buyer benefit:** the line must state one concrete benefit (*buy this product and get this exact benefit*), not vague quality praise.
2. **Competitive exclusivity:** verify against dated competitor observations that the primary alternatives either cannot deliver it or do not currently claim it. If a rival claims it with proof, it fails this test and is marked *shared*.
3. **Purchasing force:** buyer research (`customer.md`) must show target buyers care enough about that difference to switch or choose you.

Reeves's wear-out discipline: once a validated USP works, keep repeating the same core proposition across campaigns instead of reinventing the message every quarter.
