# Category design: tests, parts and traps

## Why it is a decision first

Play Bigger's argument is that the company that defines a category and is seen as its leader tends to take most of its value. The cost is that a new category starts with no searches, no budget line and no buyer who knows to look for it: the business has to create the demand. The spec's own principle for Market applies: sell into existing demand; creating demand is slow and expensive. So the test comes before the name.

## The five conditions

| Condition | Met looks like | Not met looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A felt problem no category owns | buyers describe a problem in their own words that neither the business's category nor the alternatives' categories solve | buyers' problems map neatly onto an existing category |
| The current frame costs deals | lost deals or objections caused by being filed in the wrong category (*you looked like X*) | losses are about price, timing or fit, not the frame |
| A real difference to anchor it | a differentiator or mechanism that exists today and the alternatives lack | the difference is proposed, shared, or only a new name |
| Demand signal | buyers search for or ask about the problem; call questions use its language | no search or call evidence; or every search uses the old category's terms |
| Capacity to educate and serve | time, budget and team for sustained market education; delivery capacity for the demand it creates | unknown budget, a small team, capped delivery capacity |

Three or more *met* with the difference condition among them is the minimum case for a new category. Otherwise prefer a subcategory.

## Subcategory first

A subcategory keeps the demand of the existing category and narrows the frame: *bookkeeping for creative agencies*, *the finance team for agencies*, or a named problem inside an existing category. It can later grow into a category if the conditions start to be met. This is usually the cheaper first move.

## The parts

- **Problem:** named in the buyer's language. Play Bigger's point is that the problem comes before the solution: if buyers don't feel it, nothing else works.
- **Category name:** clear before clever. A buyer should guess what is in it. Compare candidates with the terms buyers actually search and say. Untested and not cleared until tested and checked.
- **Point of view (POV):** the old way, why it fails now, the new way, and why this business delivers it. It is the story the whole company tells; it uses only differences that exist.
- **Lightning strike:** a concentrated launch moment that puts the POV in front of the market at once. Its date, budget and media are the owner's decisions.
- **Category king signals:** buyers and others use the term, search for it, name the business first. Each should be measurable.

## Traps

- **Name first.** A clever category name with no felt problem behind it is a tagline.
- **Famous stories as evidence.** Well-known category creations are illustrations of the idea, not evidence that it will work here or of how long it takes.
- **Inventing demand.** Search volumes, market sizes or trends not in the inputs.
- **Assuming a budget.** Category design without a known education budget is a hope; say *unknown* and make the test cheap.
- **Abandoning the demand you have.** Moving all messaging to a new term no buyer searches for can lose the buyers who search the old terms today.
- **Owner's decision as evidence.** If the owner has decided, record it as their decision and still show the evidence for and against.
