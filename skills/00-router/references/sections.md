## Q1. Who are we selling to, in what market?

Most marketing failures are decided here, before a word is written: the wrong market, a customer you do not understand, or a message aimed at the wrong awareness level. Three sections own this question.

### 1. Market

**Job:** choose a market where demand exists, you can win, and the economics work.

**Components:** market selection · demand (existing vs created) · market size (TAM / SAM / SOM) · competition and alternatives · category maturity · market sophistication · trends and timing · regulation · unit economics of the market (what customers can pay).

**Principles:** a starving crowd beats a great product (Halbert). Sell into existing demand; creating demand is slow and expensive. The more sophisticated the market, the less a plain claim works (Schwartz). Enter where you can be first or different, not better.

**Frameworks and methods:**

| Framework | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Schwartz's 5 stages of market sophistication | Tells you whether a direct claim, a bigger claim, a mechanism, a better mechanism, or identification will work |
| Porter's Five Forces | Rates how hard it is to make money in a category |
| Blue Ocean (Kim & Mauborgne) | Find uncontested space by changing the factors a category competes on |
| TAM / SAM / SOM | Size the market in three rings so you do not confuse ambition with reach |
| Hormozi's Starving Crowd test | Pain, purchasing power, easy to target, growing |
| Ansoff Matrix (owned in Growth) | Existing vs new product × existing vs new market: four growth routes |
| PESTLE | Political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental forces |
| Demand analysis | Search volume, ad-spend data, competitor count, pricing bands |
| Category Design (Play Bigger, Ramadan et al.; owned in Positioning) | Create and own a new category rather than compete in an old one |

### 2. Customer

**Job:** know the buyer better than they know themselves, in their own words.

**Components:** customer research · segmentation · ideal customer profile (ICP) · personas · Jobs to Be Done · pains, desires, fears, motivations · beliefs and objections · buying triggers · voice of customer (VoC) · buying process and decision makers (B2B) · current alternatives · identity and self-image.

**Principles:** people buy to move from a current state to a desired state (Hormozi, Sugarman). They hire products to do a job (Christensen). Decisions are emotional first, justified rationally after (Kahneman). The customer's words beat your words: copy is assembled from research, not invented. A niche you can describe precisely beats a market you cannot.

**Frameworks and methods:**

| Framework | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Jobs to Be Done (Christensen, Ulwick, Klement) | Functional, emotional and social jobs; the progress the customer wants |
| ICP / persona | Who buys, who decides, who pays, who uses |
| Segmentation (demographic, psychographic, behavioral, needs-based, firmographic) | Split a market into groups that respond differently |
| STP (Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning) | Kotler's sequence; positioning is finished in section 6 |
| Voice of Customer mining | Reviews, interviews, surveys, support tickets, forums, sales calls |
| Customer interviews (The Mom Test, Fitzpatrick) | Ask about past behavior, never hypotheticals |
| Dream Buyer / Dream 100 (Holmes, Brunson; owned in Distribution) | Define the exact buyer and where they already gather |
| Before–After grid (Digital Marketer) | Have, feel, average day, status: before vs after |
| Empathy map | Think, feel, say, do, pains, gains |
| Buyer psychology (Cialdini, Kahneman, Ariely, Fogg) | Reciprocity, scarcity, authority, consistency, liking, social proof, unity, loss aversion, anchoring, motivation–ability–prompt |
| Belief mapping (false beliefs; Epiphany Bridge owned in Message) | The vehicle, internal and external beliefs that block the sale |
| Maslow / core desires (Whitman's Life-Force 8) | Survival, food, safety, sex, comfort, status, care for loved ones, social approval |

### 3. Awareness

**Job:** match what you say to what the buyer already knows.

**Components:** Schwartz's five levels (unaware · problem aware · solution aware · product aware · most aware) · customer journey (See, Think, Do, Care · awareness, consideration, decision, retention) · buying temperature (cold, warm, hot) · intent (informational, navigational, commercial, transactional).

**Principles:** lead with what the buyer already believes, then take them one step forward. Unaware buyers need a story or an identity hook; most-aware buyers need the deal. A message two levels ahead of the reader is ignored. Each level needs its own ad, page and offer.

**Frameworks and methods:**

| Framework | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Schwartz's 5 Levels of Awareness | Decides headline type, length and proof needed |
| Awareness × Sophistication grid | Combines level and market sophistication to choose the angle |
| Customer journey map | Touchpoints and emotions at each stage |
| See–Think–Do–Care (Google) | Intent-based journey for content and ads |
| Buyer's journey (HubSpot) | Awareness, consideration, decision |
| Traffic temperature (cold, warm, hot) | Which traffic gets which page and offer |
| Search intent classification | Informational, navigational, commercial, transactional |

## Q2. What are we selling, and why would they choose it?

The offer decides the economics of everything downstream: a weak offer makes great copy and cheap traffic irrelevant, a strong one forgives both. Four sections own this question: the thing itself, the memory it builds, the place it holds in the mind, and the deal.

### 4. Product

**Job:** make sure the thing being sold actually delivers the result, before marketing amplifies it.

**Components:** product–market fit · core result · delivery mechanism · quality and consistency · product roadmap · packaging and tiers · product-led growth · product marketing (launches, feature messaging).

**Principles:** marketing a product that does not deliver only speeds up the refunds. Fit is measured by retention and word of mouth, not by sales. The product is the first marketing channel: a result worth talking about is distribution.

**Frameworks and methods:**

| Framework | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Product–Market Fit (Andreessen, Rachleff) | The market pulls the product out of you; sales feel easy |
| Sean Ellis PMF survey | 40%+ of users would be very disappointed without it |
| 4Ps (McCarthy) | Product, Price, Place, Promotion: the classic mix; Price lives in Offer, Place in Distribution |
| 7Ps (Booms & Bitner) | Adds People, Process, Physical evidence for services |
| Lean Startup (Ries) | MVP → build, measure, learn |
| Kano model | Basic, performance and delight features |
| Product-Led Growth (Bush) | Free product as the acquisition and conversion engine |
| Hook Model (Eyal) | Trigger, action, variable reward, investment: habit-forming products |
| Product launch formulas (Walker's PLF, owned in Funnel; Brunson's Perfect Webinar, owned in Copywriting) | Sequenced release that builds anticipation and proof |

### 5. Brand

**Job:** be remembered and preferred before the buyer is ready, so that when they are, you are the first and easiest choice.

**Components:** brand identity (name, visual, voice) · distinctive assets · brand promise · brand story · personality and values · mental availability (being thought of) · physical availability (being easy to find and buy) · reputation and trust · brand vs performance budget split.

**Principles:** brands grow by reaching more buyers and being easy to think of, not by deepening loyalty (Sharp). Distinctive beats different: be recognizable before you are persuasive. Brand is what people remember when the ad is over; performance is what they do when it is on. Over a long horizon, on average 60% brand / 40% activation outperforms either alone (Binet & Field; the ratio shifts by category).

**Frameworks and methods:**

| Framework | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| How Brands Grow (Sharp, Ehrenberg-Bass) | Mental and physical availability, distinctive assets, penetration over loyalty |
| The Long and the Short of It (Binet & Field) | Brand-building vs sales activation, time horizons, budget split |
| Keller's CBBE pyramid | Salience → performance and imagery → judgments and feelings → resonance |
| Aaker's brand equity model | Awareness, associations, perceived quality, loyalty |
| Brand archetypes (Mark & Pearson) | Twelve personality patterns: hero, sage, outlaw, caregiver… |
| Brand key / brand pyramid | One-page brand definition: root strength, insight, benefits, values, personality, essence |
| Golden Circle (Sinek) | Why, how, what |
| StoryBrand (Miller) | Customer is the hero, brand is the guide |
| Distinctive asset audit | Which colors, shapes, sounds, characters are uniquely yours |
| Share of voice vs share of market (Jones, Binet & Field) | Excess share of voice predicts growth |

### 6. Positioning

**Job:** own one clear place in the buyer's mind relative to the alternatives.

**Components:** category (what are you?) · target customer (for whom?) · competitive alternatives (instead of what?) · differentiation (what do you have they lack?) · unique mechanism (why does it work?) · USP · value proposition · proof of difference · reframing the category.

**Principles:** positioning is not what you do to the product; it is what you do to the mind (Ries & Trout). First is better than better. If you are not first, create a category you can be first in. A position is a trade-off: who you are *not* for is part of it. The unique mechanism is the single strongest reason-to-believe in sophisticated markets.

**Frameworks and methods:**

| Framework | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Positioning (Ries & Trout) | Own a word in the mind; ladders and the law of leadership |
| Obviously Awesome (Dunford) | Alternatives → unique attributes → value → best-fit customers → category |
| Positioning statement (Moore, Crossing the Chasm) | For \[customer\] who \[need\], \[product\] is a \[category\] that \[benefit\]; unlike \[alternative\], it \[difference\] |
| Unique Selling Proposition (Reeves) | One specific benefit the competition cannot or does not claim |
| Unique mechanism (Schwartz, Bencivenga, Kennedy) | The named reason the result happens: the method, ingredient or process |
| Value Proposition Canvas (Osterwalder) | Jobs, pains, gains ↔ products, pain relievers, gain creators |
| Category Design (Play Bigger, Ramadan et al.) | Name and own the problem, the category and the solution |
| Perceptual map | Two axes buyers care about; where you and alternatives sit |
| Only-ness statement (Neumeier) | Our brand is the only \[category\] that \[difference\] for \[customer\] in \[market\] who \[need\] during \[era\] |
| Competitive alternatives analysis | What they would do if you did not exist (often: nothing, or a spreadsheet) |

### 7. Offer

**Job:** make what they get worth clearly more than what they give up, and make choosing it feel safe and urgent.

**Components:** desired outcome · value (dream outcome, likelihood, time, effort) · deliverables and packaging · price and pricing model · bonuses · guarantee and risk reversal · urgency · scarcity · payment terms · naming · reason to buy · the core offer vs front-end vs back-end.

**Principles:** people buy when perceived value exceeds price by a wide margin (Hormozi). Sell the destination, not the vehicle: nobody wants the modules, they want what happens after. Price is a signal, not just a number; raising price can raise perceived value. Every risk you remove is a sale you did not lose. Urgency and scarcity only work when they are true. Charge for the outcome, deliver the method.

**Frameworks and methods:**

| Framework | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Value Equation (Hormozi) | (Dream outcome × perceived likelihood) ÷ (time delay × effort and sacrifice) |
| Grand Slam Offer (Hormozi) | Problems → solutions → delivery vehicles → trim and stack → name, bonuses, guarantee, scarcity, urgency |
| Irresistible Offer (Joyner) | High ROI, believability, touchstone (the one-line hook) |
| Value Ladder (Brunson) | Free → low-ticket → core → high-ticket → continuity |
| Offer stack (Brunson's Stack slide) | Itemize everything, assign value, anchor the total against the price |
| Risk reversal (Abraham) | Guarantees that shift risk from buyer to seller: money-back, results-based, better-than-money-back |
| Pricing strategy | Cost-plus, value-based, competitor-based, penetration, premium, tiered, usage, subscription |
| Price psychology | Anchoring, decoy effect, charm pricing, price–quality signal, payment plans |
| Van Westendorp / Gabor-Granger | Survey methods for price sensitivity and willingness to pay |
| Bonuses that kill objections (Hormozi) | Each bonus solves the next objection in line |
| Tripwire / self-liquidating offer (Digital Marketer) | Low-price front end that pays for acquisition |
| Continuity and recurring offers | Memberships, subscriptions, retainers |
| Naming formula (Hormozi's MAGIC) | Magnetic reason why, Avatar, Goal, Interval, Container |
| Best-prospect offer test | Would your 100 best prospects say yes without a sales call? |

## Q3. What do we say?

The message is the offer translated into the buyer's language at their awareness level; copy is only the last mile. Three sections own this question, from the big idea down to the sentence.

### 8. Message

**Job:** decide the one idea, the promise, and the reasons that make it believable, before any ad or page is written.

**Components:** big idea · promise (the headline claim) · problem framing · desired outcome · mechanism (how it works) · reason why · reason to believe · proof (results, testimonials, data, demonstrations, credentials, logos) · objection handling · enemy / villain · story · identity and belonging · messaging hierarchy (primary, secondary, supporting).

**Principles:** one idea, one emotion, one story, one action (the Rule of One). Give a reason why: a claim with a reason outperforms the bare claim (Hopkins, Cialdini). Reason to buy and reason to believe are two different jobs; most weak messaging has the first without the second. Proof beats promise in sophisticated markets. Specific beats general: a number, a name, a date. Enter the conversation already in the buyer's head (Collier). The best message is usually found in customer research, not invented.

**Frameworks and methods:**

| Framework | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Big Idea (Ogilvy, Bernbach) | One surprising, emotionally charged, relevant idea the whole campaign hangs on |
| Reason Why (Hopkins, Scientific Advertising) | Explain why the product does what it does and why the deal exists |
| Reason to Believe (RTB) | Proof points under each claim |
| Rule of One (Masterson & Forde) | One idea, one emotion, one story, one action |
| Unique mechanism (Schwartz, Bencivenga, Kennedy; owned in Positioning) | The named cause of the result, deployed here |
| 4Ps of persuasion (taught by AWAI) | Promise, Picture, Proof, Push |
| 4Us (Masterson; owned in Copywriting) | Useful, Urgent, Unique, Ultra-specific |
| Messaging hierarchy / message house | Roof (core promise), pillars (three supporting messages), foundation (proof) |
| Enemy / common enemy (Brunson, Kennedy) | A villain or false belief the buyer and brand oppose together |
| Epiphany Bridge (Brunson) | The story of your own discovery, told to rebuild the buyer's beliefs |
| Objection map | Every reason not to buy, paired with the answer and the proof |
| Proof stack | Results, testimonials, case studies, data, demos, authority, social proof, borrowed credibility |
| Hero's Journey / StoryBrand (StoryBrand owned in Brand) | Story structure applied to the customer as hero |
| Emotional vs rational benefit ladder (laddering) | Feature → functional benefit → emotional benefit → value |
| Sticky ideas: SUCCESs (Heath) | Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories |

### 9. Creative Strategy

**Job:** turn the message into ideas that stop the scroll for a specific audience at a specific awareness level, in a specific format.

**Components:** audience segment · awareness stage · angle · concept · hook · format (static, video, UGC, carousel, long-form, native) · visual · copy · CTA · creative volume and testing cadence · creative brief · creative fatigue.

**Principles:** the angle is the strategy; the ad is just the execution. One offer has many angles: pain, desire, fear, curiosity, identity, status, enemy, mechanism, proof, price. Volume wins in paid social: more concepts tested beats one polished ad. Hook first: the first three seconds or first line decide whether the rest exists. Match the format to the platform's native behavior. Creative is the biggest lever in paid media once targeting is automated.

**Frameworks and methods:**

| Framework | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Creative brief | Audience, awareness, insight, single message, proof, tone, format, CTA, mandatories |
| Angle matrix | Awareness levels × emotional drivers × formats; every cell is a test |
| Hook–Story–Offer (Brunson) | The three parts of every ad, email and page |
| Hook library | Question, bold claim, contrarian, curiosity gap, pattern interrupt, callout, number, story open |
| Creative testing ladder (owned in Experimentation) | New concepts, then variations of winners, then iterations; scale winners, refresh before fatigue |
| UGC and creator frameworks | Problem-solution, testimonial, unboxing, day-in-the-life, before-after |
| Native ad principle | Make the ad look and feel like the content around it |
| Visual hierarchy | Where the eye goes first, second, third |
| Message–market–media match (Kennedy) | Right message, right market, right medium |

### 10. Copywriting

**Job:** write words that move the reader to the next action. This is where the small, famous frameworks live.

**Components:** headlines · leads and openers · body copy · bullets and fascinations · subheads · CTAs · P.S. · email copy · ad copy · landing-page copy · sales-page copy · video sales letters (VSL) · scripts · microcopy · editing.

**Principles:** the headline is 80% of the result (Ogilvy, Caples). Every sentence exists to get the next one read (Sugarman's slippery slide). Write to one person in their language. Specificity sells; adjectives do not. Benefits over features, outcomes over benefits. Long copy outperforms short when the reader is interested and the price is high. The P.S. is the second-most-read line. Write first, edit ruthlessly; cut every word that does not earn its place.

**Structure frameworks:**

| Framework | Structure |
| --- | --- |
| AIDA | Attention, Interest, Desire, Action |
| PAS | Problem, Agitate, Solve |
| PASTOR (Ray Edwards) | Problem, Amplify, Story, Transformation, Offer, Response |
| BAB | Before, After, Bridge |
| FAB | Features, Advantages, Benefits |
| 4Ps of persuasion (taught by AWAI; owned in Message) | Promise, Picture, Proof, Push |
| 4Cs | Clear, Concise, Compelling, Credible |
| QUEST | Qualify, Understand, Educate, Stimulate, Transition |
| ACCA | Awareness, Comprehension, Conviction, Action |
| SLAP | Stop, Look, Act, Purchase |
| Star–Story–Solution | Introduce the star, tell their story, present the solution |
| Perfect Webinar (Brunson) | Big domino → three secrets (vehicle, internal, external beliefs) → stack and close |
| Problem–Promise–Proof–Proposal | Direct-response page skeleton |
| Long-form sales letter structure (Halbert, Kennedy's Ultimate Sales Letter) | Classic long-form letter sequences |
| StoryBrand BrandScript | Character, problem, guide, plan, call to action, failure, success |

**Headline and lead frameworks:**

| Framework | Structure |
| --- | --- |
| Bly's headline types | Direct, indirect, news, how-to, question, command, reason-why, testimonial |
| 4Us (Masterson) | Useful, Urgent, Unique, Ultra-specific |
| Great Leads (Masterson & Forde) | Offer, Promise, Problem–Solution, Big Secret, Proclamation, Story: chosen by awareness level |
| Kennedy's headline formulas | Who else wants…, How I…, Warning:…, The secret of… |
| Schwartz's headline-by-awareness | Most aware: product + price; aware: claim; unaware: story or identity |
| Open loops / curiosity gaps | Withhold the resolution to keep reading |

**Techniques:** specificity · future pacing · damaging admission · takeaway selling · stacking proof · fascinations (bullets that tease) · power words · rhythm and sentence length · you-focused language · urgency and scarcity lines · the one-two punch of promise and proof · reading-level control · the bucket brigade ("Here's the thing:") · P.S. · testimonials woven into copy.

**Formats:** sales page · landing page · email (welcome, nurture, launch, cart, win-back) · ad (short, long, story) · VSL · webinar script · product description · social post · script for sales calls · direct mail · press release.

**Masters to study:** Hopkins, Caples, Ogilvy, Schwartz, Collier, Halbert, Sugarman, Bencivenga, Carlton, Kennedy, Bly, Masterson, Ray Edwards, Brunson, Hormozi.

## Q4. How do we reach them?

Attention has to be bought, earned or borrowed, then routed through a path that turns it into a sale. Two sections own this question: where the traffic comes from, and the structure it flows through.

### 11. Distribution

**Job:** put the message in front of the right people, at a cost that works, in channels you can grow.

**Components:** paid (search, social, display, video, native, affiliate, influencer) · organic (SEO, content, social, community, PR, word of mouth) · owned (email, SMS, push, app, community) · earned (press, reviews, referrals, UGC) · partnerships · affiliates and JVs · direct outreach (cold email, DMs, calls) · events and speaking · marketplaces · offline (direct mail, OOH, TV, radio, print) · channel mix and sequencing.

**Principles:** one channel done well beats five done badly; most companies grow on one or two channels (Weinberg & Mares). Go where the customer already is; do not try to move them. Paid buys speed, organic buys compounding, owned buys independence. Every channel has a native format and a native cost: respect both. Borrow audiences before you build them (Dream 100, partnerships). Rent-to-own: convert paid and borrowed attention into owned (email, community).

**Frameworks and methods:**

| Framework | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Bullseye Framework (Traction, Weinberg & Mares) | Brainstorm 19 channels, test 3, focus on 1 |
| Paid–Owned–Earned (POEM) | Classify media by who controls it |
| Dream 100 (Holmes, Brunson) | Work your way into the audiences your buyers already follow |
| Traffic temperature (Brunson; owned in Awareness) | Cold, warm, hot traffic → different ads and pages |
| Content marketing (Pulizzi, Handley) | Pillar content, clusters, repurposing, distribution |
| SEO | Keyword research, intent, on-page, technical, links, topical authority |
| Local SEO | Google Business Profile, Map Pack (relevance, distance, prominence), NAP citations, local pages |
| Paid search (Google, Bing) | Intent capture: keywords, match types, quality score, bidding |
| Shopping & product feeds | Google Merchant Center, feed-as-targeting, supplemental titles, custom-label SKU segmentation |
| Paid social (Meta, TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn, Pinterest, Reddit) | Interruption & visual/community feeds: creative-led, broad targeting, algorithm learning |
| Marketplace & retail media | Amazon Ads, TikTok Shop, Walmart/Etsy: retail readiness before spend, Buy Box, ACoS vs TACoS |
| WhatsApp & conversational messaging | Explicit WhatsApp opt-in, Utility vs Marketing templates, 24-hour service window, quality rating |
| Influencer and creator marketing | Seeding, sponsorship, affiliate, whitelisting |
| Affiliate and JV launches | Pay on performance; partners promote in exchange for commission |
| Account-Based Marketing (ABM) | Target named accounts with coordinated outreach (B2B) |
| Demand generation vs lead generation | Create demand in the market vs capture existing demand |
| Cold outreach frameworks | List → personalized first line → value → soft ask → follow-up cadence |
| PR and earned media | Newsjacking, data stories, HARO, founder story |
| Community-led growth (owned in Referral and Community) | Build a place people return to; the product sits inside |
| Flywheel (HubSpot; owned in Growth) | Attract, engage, delight: customers power acquisition |
| Omnichannel sequencing | Which channel introduces, which converts, which retains |

### 12. Funnel

**Job:** design the path from first touch to purchase (and beyond) so each step earns the next one.

**Components:** acquisition · lead magnet · landing page · lead capture · nurture sequence · sales page / sales call · checkout · order bump · upsell and downsell · thank-you page · follow-up · retargeting · onboarding hand-off · back-end offers · continuity.

**Principles:** every step has one job and one CTA. The funnel is the offer sequence in time: value ladder on a timeline. Front end acquires, back end profits (Kennedy, Abraham). Each page should be able to pay for itself. The money is in the follow-up: most buyers do not buy on first contact. Reduce steps for cold traffic, add steps for high-ticket.

**Frameworks and methods:**

| Framework | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| AARRR / Pirate Metrics (McClure) | Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue |
| Value Ladder (Brunson; owned in Offer) | Ascend buyers through increasing value and price |
| Funnel types (Brunson) | Lead (squeeze, quiz), unboxing (tripwire, cart, challenge), presentation (VSL, webinar), phone (application, call) |
| Customer Value Optimization (Digital Marketer) | Lead magnet → tripwire → core offer → profit maximizer → return path |
| Full-funnel: TOFU–MOFU–BOFU | Top, middle, bottom of funnel content and offers |
| Lead magnet design | Specific, fast, high perceived value, leads to the core offer (Hormozi's $100M Leads) |
| Webinar / challenge / launch funnels | Event-based conversion with built-in urgency |
| Application funnel | Qualify high-ticket buyers before a call |
| Email nurture: Soap Opera Sequence, Seinfeld emails (Brunson) | Story-driven sequence then daily broadcast |
| Product Launch Formula (Walker) | Sideways sales letter: pre-launch content sequence, open cart, close cart |
| Retargeting ladder | Visitors → engaged → cart → purchasers, each with its own message |
| Growth loops (Reforge; owned in Growth) | Output of one cycle feeds the input of the next; funnels that compound |
| Bowtie funnel | Acquisition on the left, expansion and advocacy on the right |

## Q5. How do they decide?

The decision happens on a page or in a conversation, and both are won by removing doubt and friction rather than adding pressure. Two sections own this question.

### 13. Conversion

**Job:** turn the visitors you already have into leads and buyers, without buying more traffic.

**Components:** conversion rate optimization (CRO) · UX and page design · page speed · trust signals · friction reduction · form design · checkout optimization · objection removal on page · social proof placement · pricing page · cart abandonment recovery · personalization · mobile experience.

**Principles:** clarity beats cleverness; the visitor should know what it is, who it is for, and what to do in five seconds. Reduce friction before adding persuasion. Every field in a form costs conversions. Trust is built by proof, consistency and design quality, not by claims of trustworthiness. Test big changes (offer, headline, layout) before small ones (button colors). Doubling conversion halves CAC: CRO is usually cheaper than more traffic.

**Frameworks and methods:**

| Framework | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| LIFT model (WiderFunnel) | Value proposition, relevance, clarity, urgency, anxiety, distraction |
| Fogg Behavior Model | Behavior = Motivation × Ability × Prompt; raise ability before motivation |
| MECLABS conversion sequence | C = 4m + 3v + 2(i−f) − 2a: motivation, value, incentive, friction, anxiety |
| Landing-page anatomy | Headline, subhead, hero, proof, benefits, mechanism, offer, FAQ, guarantee, CTA |
| Five-second test | Can a stranger say what the page sells? |
| Heuristic evaluation (Nielsen) | Ten usability rules |
| Trust stack | Logos, reviews, guarantees, security badges, real people, specific numbers |
| Checkout optimization | Guest checkout, fewer fields, progress bar, multiple payment options, order bump |
| Cart recovery sequence | Email/SMS within 1 hour, 24 hours, 72 hours, with escalating reason to return |
| A/B testing (owned in Experimentation) | Conversion is the main consumer |
| Cialdini's principles on page (owned in Customer) | Social proof, scarcity, authority, consistency, reciprocity, liking, unity |
| Behavioral nudges | Defaults, anchoring, decoy pricing, loss framing |
| Personalization | By source, awareness, segment, returning vs new |

### 14. Sales

**Job:** close higher-ticket or complex deals in conversation, where a page cannot do the whole job.

**Components:** lead qualification · discovery · sales script · demo · objection handling · closing · follow-up · pipeline management · sales–marketing alignment · DM selling · phone and video selling · proposals · negotiation.

**Principles:** diagnose before you prescribe: the prospect sells themselves when they state the gap out loud. Questions beat pitches. Sell the outcome, handle the objections, then ask. Price objections are usually value or trust objections. Speed to lead matters: respond in minutes, not days. Every no is information for the Offer and Message sections.

**Frameworks and methods:**

| Framework | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| SPIN Selling (Rackham) | Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff questions |
| Challenger Sale (Dixon & Adamson) | Teach, tailor, take control |
| MEDDIC / MEDDPICC | Metrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria, Decision process, Identify pain, Champion (+ Paper process, Competition) |
| BANT | Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline |
| Sandler Selling System | Pain funnel, up-front contracts, budget, decision, fulfillment |
| Gap Selling (Keenan) | Current state vs future state; sell the gap |
| Straight Line (Belfort) | Control the conversation along one line toward the close |
| Hormozi's CLOSER | Clarify, Label, Overview, Sell the vacation, Explain concerns, Reinforce |
| Consultative / solution selling | Diagnose, prescribe, prove |
| Objection-handling patterns | Feel–felt–found, isolate and resolve, reframe, pre-handle in the pitch |
| DM / chat selling scripts | Open, qualify, invite, close in messaging apps |
| Setter–closer model | Separate qualification from closing |
| Proposal and pricing presentation | Anchor high, three options, remove risk, define next step |

## Q6. What happens after?

Profit lives after the first sale: a customer who stays, buys again and brings a friend is worth several new ones. Two sections own this question.

### 15. Retention

**Job:** get the customer to the promised result, keep them, and grow what they spend.

**Components:** onboarding · activation (the first moment of value) · customer success · lifecycle email and SMS · habit and usage · repeat purchase · upsell and cross-sell · subscription and churn management · win-back · loyalty programs · customer support as marketing · feedback loops back into Product and Offer.

**Principles:** retention is the truest measure of product–market fit. The first week decides the first year: onboard to the result, not to the features. It costs far less to keep a customer than to win one. Churn is a symptom; the cause is usually in Product, Offer or expectation set by Message. Expansion revenue (upsells, cross-sells) is the cheapest growth there is. Treat the thank-you page and the first email as the start of the next sale.

**Frameworks and methods:**

| Framework | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Activation / aha moment | The action that predicts long-term retention; get every user there fast |
| Onboarding sequence | Welcome, quick win, deeper use, habit, upgrade |
| Lifecycle marketing (email/SMS) | Post-purchase, replenishment, win-back, anniversary (welcome and nurture live in Funnel) |
| Cohort retention analysis (owned in Measurement) | Retention curves by signup month; flat curve = fit |
| Customer success playbooks | Health scores, check-ins, QBRs, renewal motions (B2B) |
| Hook Model (Eyal; owned in Product) | Trigger → action → variable reward → investment |
| RFM segmentation | Recency, Frequency, Monetary value: who to reactivate, who to reward |
| Churn analysis | Voluntary vs involuntary, reasons, timing, save offers |
| Loyalty program design | Points, tiers, perks, VIP access, surprise and delight |
| Expansion playbook | Upsell triggers, cross-sell maps, usage-based upgrades |
| Net Promoter Score (Reichheld) | Would you recommend us? Promoters minus detractors |
| Customer effort score / CSAT | Friction and satisfaction after each interaction |
| Continuity and retention offers (Kennedy; continuity owned in Offer) | Membership, auto-ship, annual prepay, pause instead of cancel |

### 16. Referral and Community

**Job:** turn customers into the acquisition channel.

**Components:** referral programs · word of mouth · reviews and testimonials · case studies · user-generated content · community (groups, forums, events) · ambassadors and advocates · affiliate customers · virality and sharing mechanics · network effects.

**Principles:** people share what makes them look good, feel part of something, or helps someone they care about (Berger). Ask for the referral at the moment of delight, not at random. Make sharing the default path, not an afterthought. Community turns a product into a place; places are hard to leave. Reviews are copy you did not have to write: collect them systematically.

**Frameworks and methods:**

| Framework | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Contagious: STEPPS (Berger) | Social currency, Triggers, Emotion, Public, Practical value, Stories |
| Referral program design | Two-sided reward, timing, ask script, tracking |
| Viral loop / K-factor | Invites per user × conversion rate; above 1 the product spreads itself |
| Review generation system | Ask at the delight moment, make it one click, respond to every review |
| Testimonial and case-study collection | Before, after, specific result, objection it overcomes |
| UGC programs | Hashtags, contests, creator seeding, feature customers |
| Community-led growth (Spinks) | Define purpose, members, rituals, value exchange; measure engagement, not size |
| Ambassador / advocate programs | Identify promoters, give them status and tools |
| Network effects (Currier) | Direct, indirect, data, marketplace: value grows with users |
| Customer advisory boards | Formal input and loyalty from top customers (B2B) |

## Q7. How do we know, and how do we improve?

Without measurement the other sections are opinions; without experimentation they never get better; without a growth model they never scale. Three sections own this question.

### 17. Measurement

**Job:** know what is working, what it costs, and what it returns, at every stage.

**Components:** tracking (pixels, server-side, UTM, CRM) · attribution · KPIs by stage · CAC · LTV · LTV:CAC · payback period · ROAS and MER · CVR at each step · AOV · churn and retention rate · gross margin and contribution margin · dashboards · reporting cadence · incrementality.

**Principles:** measure the whole funnel, not the last click. One north-star metric, a few input metrics, everything else is diagnostic. Unit economics decide whether to scale: LTV must cover CAC with margin and inside a payback window you can finance. Attribution is always a model, never the truth; use incrementality tests for the big decisions. Vanity metrics (views, followers) are inputs at best. Track early (leads, trials) and late (revenue, retention) metrics together, or you optimize for the wrong one.

**Frameworks and methods:**

| Framework | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| North Star Metric + input metrics (Amplitude, Reforge) | The one number that captures value delivered, and the levers that move it |
| AARRR metrics (owned in Funnel) | A KPI per funnel stage |
| Unit economics model | CAC, LTV, payback, contribution margin, LTV:CAC (3:1 rule of thumb) |
| Attribution models | First touch, last touch, linear, time decay, position-based, data-driven |
| Marketing Mix Modeling (MMM) | Statistical attribution across channels, including offline |
| Incrementality / geo-lift / holdout tests | What would have happened without the spend |
| MER (Marketing Efficiency Ratio) | Total revenue ÷ total marketing spend; blended, resistant to attribution loss |
| Cohort analysis | Behavior of groups over time; the only honest retention view |
| Funnel analytics | Step-by-step CVR and drop-off |
| OKRs (owned in Operations) / KPI trees | Objectives → key results → metrics; or a driver tree from revenue down |
| Dashboard design | One screen per decision-maker; red/green thresholds; weekly cadence |
| Customer research metrics | NPS, CSAT, CES, PMF score |
| Content and SEO metrics | Impressions, clicks, rankings, assisted conversions |

### 18. Experimentation

**Job:** replace opinions with evidence, in order of expected impact.

**Components:** hypothesis · prioritization · test design · sample size and significance · A/B and multivariate · creative testing · offer and price testing · channel testing · learning log · iteration cadence · test velocity.

**Principles:** test the biggest levers first: offer, audience, message, then page, then details. A test without a hypothesis teaches nothing. Most tests lose; the win rate is low and the learning rate is the point. Set the stopping rule before the test starts (fixed sample, sequential or Bayesian) and stop only by that rule. Document every result: the losses are where the insight is. Speed of iteration compounds more than any single win.

**Frameworks and methods:**

| Framework | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Hypothesis format | If we \[change\], then \[metric\] will \[move\] because \[insight\] |
| ICE / PIE / RICE scoring | ICE: impact, confidence, ease. PIE: potential, importance, ease. RICE: reach, impact, confidence, effort |
| Build–Measure–Learn (Ries) | The core loop |
| A/B testing | Two variants, one variable, statistical significance |
| Multivariate testing | Several variables at once, high traffic needed |
| Creative testing frameworks (paid social) | Concept testing → variation testing → scaling |
| Price testing | Cohort-based, geo-based, or by offer variant |
| Minimum Viable Test | Smallest experiment that can disprove the hypothesis |
| Growth process (Ellis & Brown, Hacking Growth) | Analyze → ideate → prioritize → test, weekly |
| Learning log / experiment repository | Hypothesis, result, insight, next step |
| Sequential and Bayesian testing | Faster decisions at lower traffic |

### 19. Growth

**Job:** take what works and make it bigger without breaking it, in the right order.

**Components:** validate → test → stabilize → vertical scale → horizontal scale → monitor → optimize · scaling paid spend · adding channels · adding offers · adding segments and markets · adding products · growth loops · growth model and forecast · constraints (the bottleneck) · timing of scale.

**Principles:** stabilize before you scale; scaling an unstable system scales the instability. Vertical first (more of what works: budget, frequency, audience depth), horizontal second (new channels, offers, markets). Find the single constraint and fix only that (Theory of Constraints; Hormozi's one-constraint rule). Growth has four levers: more customers, higher price, more purchases, longer retention (Abraham's three ways, plus retention). Growth is a model, not a wish: forecast it from the funnel's numbers.

**Frameworks and methods:**

| Framework | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Growth stages: validate → test → stabilize → scale vertically → scale horizontally → monitor → optimize | The order of operations for scaling anything |
| Abraham's three ways to grow | More customers, more transactions per customer, higher value per transaction |
| Theory of Constraints (Goldratt) / Hormozi's constraint | Find the bottleneck, fix it, find the next |
| Growth loops (Reforge) | Viral, content, paid, sales loops that compound |
| Growth model / forecast | Spreadsheet of funnel rates and volumes; what moves the output |
| Ansoff Matrix | Market penetration, product development, market development, diversification |
| Scaling paid media | Budget increases, lookalikes, broad targeting, creative volume, new placements |
| Horizontal scale paths | New channel, new offer, new segment, new geography, new product |
| Crossing the Chasm (Moore) | Early adopters → early majority: change the whole go-to-market |
| Flywheel (Collins; HubSpot) | Each turn makes the next easier |
| Core–Adjacent–Transformational (Nagji & Tuff) | Portfolio of growth bets by distance from the core |

## Q8. How do we run it?

Strategy fails in execution more often than in thinking: no owner, no budget, no process, no standard. One section owns this question and it holds everything the other nineteen need to operate.

### 20. Operations

**Job:** give the system people, money, tools, process and rules so it runs every week without you.

**Components:** marketing strategy and planning (annual, quarterly, campaign) · team structure and roles · hiring and training · agencies and freelancers · budget allocation and pacing · martech stack (CRM, email, analytics, ads, CMS, automation) · processes and SOPs · creative production pipeline · content calendar · project management · standards and quality control · brand guidelines · legal and compliance (ad policies, privacy, consumer protection, claims substantiation) · documentation and knowledge base · marketing–sales–product alignment.

**Principles:** every section in this map needs an owner, a metric and a cadence, or it does not happen. Document the process once it works twice (SOPs turn skill into system). Budget follows evidence: fund what Measurement proves, test with a fixed slice, cut what fails. Standards are set by what you tolerate; "pwede na" ("good enough") is a decision. Tools do not fix a missing process. Compliance is cheaper before the launch than after the complaint.

**Frameworks and methods:**

| Framework | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Marketing plan (one page) | Market, customer, offer, message, channels, budget, KPIs, calendar |
| 70/20/10 budget rule | Proven channels, promising channels, experiments |
| RACI | Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed, per workstream |
| T-shaped marketer / pod structure | Specialists around a generalist; growth pods per funnel stage |
| SOP and playbook library | Step-by-step for every repeated task, with examples and checklists |
| Creative production pipeline | Brief → script → produce → review → launch → analyze → iterate |
| Content calendar and sprint planning | What ships when, who owns it |
| Weekly growth meeting | Metrics review, experiments, decisions, owners |
| Martech stack map | What each tool does, what it connects to, who owns it |
| Agency and freelancer management | Brief, scope, KPIs, review cadence |
| Brand and messaging guidelines | Voice, visuals, claims you can and cannot make |
| Compliance checklist | Platform ad policies, privacy law (GDPR, CCPA, Philippine Data Privacy Act), consumer-protection and advertising-standards rules, testimonial and earnings-claim disclosures |
| EOS / Scaling Up / OKRs | Company operating systems that marketing plugs into |
| Marketing council | Multi-perspective review of a plan or asset before launch |
| Post-mortem / campaign review | What happened, why, what we keep, what we change |

## Learning path

Learn in six phases, in the order the money is decided, and build at least one artifact per section for a real business as you go; reading without building produces a critic, not a marketer. Each phase lists the sections, the primary sources that defined them, and the thing you should be able to produce at the end.

| Phase | Sections | Read | Build |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Why people buy | Customer, Awareness, Offer | Breakthrough Advertising (Schwartz); $100M Offers (Hormozi); Influence (Cialdini); The Mom Test (Fitzpatrick); Competing Against Luck (Christensen); Thinking, Fast and Slow (Kahneman) | ICP and voice-of-customer file; awareness map of your buyers; one Grand Slam Offer with value equation, guarantee, bonuses, price |
| 2. What to say | Message, Copywriting, Creative Strategy | Scientific Advertising (Hopkins); Tested Advertising Methods (Caples); Ogilvy on Advertising; The Adweek Copywriting Handbook (Sugarman); The Boron Letters (Halbert); Great Leads (Masterson & Forde); Copywriting Secrets (Jim Edwards); Expert Secrets (Brunson); The Ultimate Sales Letter and No B.S. | 3. Getting it in front of people | Funnel, Distribution, Conversion | DotCom Secrets and Traffic Secrets (Brunson); $100M Leads (Hormozi); Traction (Weinberg & Mares); Content Inc. (Pulizzi); Don't Make Me Think (Krug) | Funnel map with offer per step; lead magnet; email sequence; one channel run for 90 days; landing page with a conversion checklist applied |

| 5. | 6. After the sale, and running it | Sales, Retention, Referral and Community, Operations | SPIN Selling (Rackham); The Challenger Sale (Dixon & Adamson); Way of the Wolf (Belfort); Gap Selling (Keenan); Contagious (Berger); The Business of Belonging (Spinks); Customer Success (Mehta et al.); Traction (Wickman, EOS); Measure What Matters (Doerr) | Sales script with objection answers; onboarding and lifecycle sequence; referral and review system; one-page marketing plan with owners, budget and cadence; SOP library |
