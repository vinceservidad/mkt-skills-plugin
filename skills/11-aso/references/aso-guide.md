# ASO guide

## Store funnel

Impressions → product page views → downloads → first open → active user or subscriber.

- Page views / impressions low: the icon, name and first screenshots in search results are not earning the tap, or the app shows for the wrong searches.
- Downloads / page views low: the page itself (screenshots, description, ratings) does not convince.
- Downloads high but few active: the listing promises something the first session does not deliver, or the audience is wrong.

## Listing fields (verify current limits)

Apple App Store: app name, subtitle, keyword field (not shown to users), promotional text, description, screenshots, preview video. Google Play: app title, short description, full description, graphics. Character limits, which fields are indexed for search, and the rules on keyword stuffing and competitor names are in each store's developer documentation and change. Cite developer.apple.com/app-store and the Play Console Help Center pages with URL and date, or mark *to confirm*.

## Keywords

- Start from jobs and problem words in Voice of customer and tickets, not the brand's vocabulary.
- Store analytics often do not show which search terms brought downloads; without a keyword tool, volume and difficulty are unknown. Mark them *(assumption)*.
- A one-word category term ("coffee") is crowded and mostly brand searches; a small app rarely ranks. Prefer specific phrases that match what the app does fully.
- Do not repeat the same word across fields that the store indexes together; do not use competitor brand names.

## Screenshots

- The first two are seen in search results on many devices. Show the main outcome with a short caption in the user's words.
- Never lead with a login screen, settings or an empty state.
- Show real screens. Captions claim only what the app does.

## Claims

"#1", "best", rankings, awards and download counts need a dated source. Store review guidelines reject misleading metadata; cite the current guideline section or mark *to confirm*.

## Ratings and reviews

- Use the platform's in-app review prompt (Apple and Google limit how often it shows; cite the current rule).
- Ask after a success moment (a task completed), never right after install, an error or a crash.
- Reply to reviews; when a reported bug is fixed, say so in the reply.

## Testing

Apple's product page optimization and Google Play's store listing experiments let some listing elements be tested; availability and rules change, so cite them. Otherwise change one element at a time and compare equal periods, noting seasonality.
