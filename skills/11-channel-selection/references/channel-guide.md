## Ranking the middle ring

Score each candidate on these, citing evidence:

1. **Reach:** evidence that this business's buyer is reachable there (lead sources, journey touchpoints, what customers said about where they look). A channel the business has never tested is a hypothesis.
2. **Fit:** does the channel fit the price and buying process? High-price, considered B2B purchases suit referrals, partnerships, content, search and direct outreach; low-price consumer purchases need volume channels (heuristic).
3. **Cost against the allowable acquisition cost** (below).
4. **Time to a readable result:** SEO and community compound slowly; paid and outreach read fast.
5. **Capacity:** the owner's hours and skills, the budget, and how many new customers delivery can take.
6. **Existing assets:** a channel that already produces customers is usually the first to strengthen, not abandon.

Close rates differ by source. A source with fewer leads but a much higher close rate can be worth more than a bigger one; compare on customers and value, not lead counts.

## Economics

- **Value of a customer** = monthly price × gross margin × expected lifetime in months (or per-order margin × expected orders). Use only figures in context or `inputs/`; tenure or churn must be the business's own.
- **Allowable acquisition cost** = value of a customer × the share the owner is willing to spend on acquisition (owner's decision). If the owner has not set it, show the value of a customer and ask.
- If margin, churn or tenure is missing, write the formula with the missing input named. Do not substitute revenue for margin without saying so; label any revenue-based figure as an upper bound.
- Capacity caps volume: if the business can take four new clients a month, a channel that could bring forty is not the constraint to solve.

## Tests

- Smallest version that can be read: a handful of partner conversations, two podcast guest spots, one month of a tightly scoped search campaign.
- Success metric in the business outcome: qualified calls, clients, paying subscribers who stay. Clicks, followers, opens and impressions are diagnostics.
- Keep rule and stop rule, each tied to the metric and the allowable cost (or the owner's tolerance). Label any count or time window a heuristic.
- One owner per test, and the channel skill that runs it.

## Platform claims

Ad costs, targeting options, marketplace fees, formats and policies change. Cite the platform's own current documentation with URL and date, or mark *to confirm*. Do not use third-party benchmarks as evidence about this business.

## Authorisation

The plan is a recommendation. Money, partner commissions, contracts and launches are the owner's decisions; record what was approved and verify it after launch.
