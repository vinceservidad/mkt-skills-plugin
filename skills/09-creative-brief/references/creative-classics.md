# Creative strategy classics (Bernbach, Ogilvy, Burnett)

Apply these rules inside the creative brief so designers, editors, and copywriters build memorable execution grounded in strategy and verified proof.

## 1. Honest admission and visual-verbal tension (Bernbach)

From Bill Bernbach (Doyle Dane Bernbach, *Think Small* for Volkswagen, *We Try Harder* for Avis):

- **Honest damaging admission:** look at `customer.md` (objections, current alternatives) and `offer.md` for a real, verifiable limitation buyers already notice (smaller size, higher price, limited seats, smaller market share). When true and paired with real proof, owning that limitation first makes every positive claim far more believable. Never invent a fake flaw or undermine an unapproved price/term.
- **Visual-verbal tension:** brief the image/video maker and writer together so the visual and headline do not repeat the same literal thought. Instead, let the visual show the tension, contrast, or human truth while the words resolve it into the product benefit.
- **Speak to human emotion simply:** avoid sterile corporate polish or mechanical hype; tell the designer what real buyer feeling or moment in `customer.md` the creative captures.

## 2. Research-first creative discipline (Ogilvy)

From David Ogilvy (*Ogilvy on Advertising*):

- Keep the brief centered on **the single proposition and reason to believe**, backed by specific proof IDs from `08-proof-stack`.
- Never treat originality as an excuse to hide the brand cue, drop the offer mechanics, or confuse the viewer about what is being sold.

## 3. Inherent Drama check (Burnett)

From Leo Burnett: verify that the creative concept inside the brief pulls its emotional hook directly out of the product's real use and outcome (`Brand foundation`, `Core offer`) rather than tacking on unrelated shock gimmicks or borrowed memes.
