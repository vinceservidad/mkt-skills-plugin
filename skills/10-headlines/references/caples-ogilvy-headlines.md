# Tested headline rules (Caples & Ogilvy)

Use John Caples (*Tested Advertising Methods*) and David Ogilvy (*Ogilvy on Advertising*) to draft and screen headlines after selecting the awareness lead type. Never invent a number, timeframe, or customer claim beyond `context/`.

## 1. Three-bucket appeal filter (Caples)

Every winning tested headline in Caples's split-run archives fell into one or more of three core appeals:

1. **Self-interest:** tells the buyer directly what specific outcome, saving, or gain they get (*must exist in `offer.md` or `message.md`*).
2. **News:** announces a real new method, release, pricing shift, or verified study finding (`How`, `New`, `Announcing`).
3. **Curiosity:** opens an honest curiosity gap around a real mechanism or story (*mostly for Problem-Aware and Unaware traffic, paired with a quick payoff*).

Weak headlines rely on blind puns, vague cleverness, or general claims without buyer benefit.

## 2. Tested pull rules (Caples)

- **Picture in words:** favor concrete nouns and observable actions over abstract adjectives.
- **Keep positive over empty scare tactics** unless real switching evidence (`customer.md`) shows fear of a documented failure motivates the segment.
- **Short headlines for Most-Aware offers, longer specific headlines for Product/Solution-Aware:** cut fluff words, not necessary proof or clarity.
- **Avoid "hard-to-believe" superlatives:** replace *fastest* or *best* with the verified fact or condition.

## 3. Split-run scorecard (Caples) & 5x Rule (Ogilvy)

Ogilvy emphasized that headlines draw about five times as many readers as body copy. Before finalizing the headline table:
- Verify the top three recommended test headlines differ on one clear angle or bucket (Self-interest vs. News vs. Curiosity or Direct vs. Indirect), not three minor synonym tweaks.
- Verify that every specific figure, deadline, or result inside any headline traces directly to `offer.md` or `message.md`.
- Note how conversion (leads, purchases, booked calls) decides the split test, never clicks alone.
