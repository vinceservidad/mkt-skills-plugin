# Headline and lead types

## Lead type by awareness (Great Leads; Schwartz)

| Awareness | Lead type | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Most aware | Offer | the product, the deal, the deadline (only if true) |
| Product aware | Promise | the biggest benefit, stated directly |
| Solution aware | Problem–solution or big secret | why other solutions fail, and the mechanism that doesn't |
| Problem aware | Problem–solution, proclamation | name the problem in their words, promise relief |
| Unaware | Story, proclamation | a story or a surprising statement about their world |

## Bly's headline types

direct · indirect · news · how-to · question · command · reason-why · testimonial

A direct headline states the promise outright; an indirect one reaches it by curiosity or a hint, so it must be paid off in the lead. Caples' tested types (how-to, news, question, command, reason-why, testimonial) are among these.

## Headline formulas (Kennedy)

- *Who else wants [result]?*
- *How I [result] without [pain]*
- *Warning: [threat]*
- *The secret of [result]*

## 4Us (Masterson)

Score each 1–4:
- **Useful:** a clear benefit.
- **Urgent:** a reason to read now.
- **Unique:** not interchangeable with a competitor's headline.
- **Ultra-specific:** a number, a time frame, a named reader.

## Open loops / curiosity gap

Withhold the resolution to keep the reader reading. But the copy must pay it off; never tease what the offer doesn't deliver.
