# Schwartz's five stages of market sophistication

From Breakthrough Advertising (Eugene Schwartz, 1966). Sophistication measures how many claims the market has already heard. It is about the market. Awareness measures how much one buyer knows about the problem and the product. It is about the buyer. A market can be highly sophisticated while many buyers in it are still unaware.

| Stage | Market signs | Claim that works | Example direction (generic) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. First | Few or no competitors make the claim; buyers have not heard it | The direct claim: name the need and say you meet it | *We do X for Y.* |
| 2. Second | Competitors make the same direct claim; the claim still lands | Enlarge it: bigger, faster, more specific, provable | *X in half the time, with a number you can check.* |
| 3. Claims exhausted | Enlarged claims everywhere, numbers outbid each other, buyers compare them | A new mechanism: say how it works, so the result is believable | *X happens because of a named method or component.* |
| 4. Mechanisms compete | Several providers name mechanisms | A better mechanism: easier, faster, surer, more specific to the buyer | *A mechanism built for this buyer's exact situation.* |
| 5. Scepticism | Buyers distrust every claim; ads lean on lifestyle and identity | Identification: recognise who the buyer is and what they live with; let proof and mechanism support it | *For people who …* |

## Classifying claims

| Type | Test |
| --- | --- |
| Direct | Says what it is or does, with no number or method |
| Enlarged | A bigger, faster or more specific version: a number, a time, *best*, *only* |
| Mechanism | Names how the result happens: a method, an ingredient, a feature with a reason |
| Improved mechanism | A mechanism framed as newer, easier or surer than other mechanisms |
| Identification | Speaks to the buyer's identity or life, with little or no product claim |

One headline can carry two types; classify by the one that leads.

## Placing the stage

- Count claim types across the evidence. The stage is where the market's leading claims sit, adjusted by buyer evidence.
- Buyer scepticism (*they all promised the same thing*), buyers comparing numbers, or buyers asking *how* moves the placement up.
- Differences between channels (search ads vs social), segments or regions are common. Place each when they differ.
- With fewer than three competitors' claims, one observation date, or no buyer evidence, call the placement tentative and say what would confirm it.

## Choosing the claim

- Never go back a stage: a direct claim in a stage-3 market reads as one more of the same.
- The new claim must be true and backed. A mechanism the offer does not have is a gap for `06-unique-mechanism`, not a line to write.
- Stage 5 identification still needs the mechanism and proof underneath; it changes what leads, not what is true.
- A competitor's visible claim is evidence of what buyers have heard, not of what works. Do not copy it.
