# Perfect Webinar (Brunson) and VSL structure

## Perfect Webinar

| Part | Content | Belief |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intro | hook, promise of the talk, who it's for | — |
| Big domino | the one belief that makes buying inevitable | — |
| Origin story | the Epiphany Bridge (from Story) | vehicle |
| Secret 1 | the new vehicle: why this way works | vehicle |
| Secret 2 | why you can do it | internal |
| Secret 3 | why outside obstacles won't stop it | external |
| Transition | "Can I show you how to get this?" | — |
| Stack | every element of the offer, itemised | — |
| Price and anchor | the anchor, then the price | — |
| Guarantee and close | the guarantee terms, then the call to action | — |
| Q&A | the top objections | — |

## Short VSL (5–20 minutes)

Hook → problem → story → mechanism → proof → offer → guarantee → call to action, repeated at the end.

## Star–Story–Solution VSL

Introduce the star: the character the viewer identifies with, taken from Story or the customer evidence. Tell their story: the problem, the struggle and the discovery, using only events in Story or inputs. Present the solution: the offer from `offer.md`, with its stack, price and guarantee. Use it when the origin story carries the pitch.

## Rules

Every story is true and sourced. A composite or hypothetical story is labelled as one. Results are from the proof stack, with their source.
