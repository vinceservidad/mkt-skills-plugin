# Inherent Drama (Burnett) and Distinctive Assets

Use Leo Burnett's principles alongside Ehrenberg-Bass recognition metrics (Byron Sharp, Jenni Romaniuk) when auditing or proposing distinctive brand cues. Keep every claim about current usage and competitor overlap tied to dated observations in `inputs/` or `context/market.md`.

## 1. Inherent Drama (Burnett)

From Leo Burnett's Chicago School creative discipline:

- **Find the drama inside the product itself.** Every product has an honest human reason people buy it, use it, or trust it. Pull visual symbols, characters, and story cues from that product truth rather than borrowing disconnected humor, shock visuals, or abstract lifestyle cliches.
- **Test each asset candidate:** does the cue immediately remind a category buyer of what the product actually does or delivers (like a rugged outdoor cue for hard-working gear, or warm kitchen craft for everyday breakfast food), or could it sit on any unrelated company?
- **Use warm, plain language:** pair visual cues with everyday speech buyers actually use in Voice of Customer records, never stiff corporate jargon.

## 2. Archetypal Brand Characters and Visual Icons (Burnett)

Classic icons (such as the Marlboro Man or Tony the Tiger) worked because they met three strict rules:

1. **Tied to one clear product truth:** the character or visual shorthand dramatizes the core buyer feeling or product strength from `Brand foundation`.
2. **Repeated without fatigue:** used identically across packaging, ads, and digital touchpoints over years rather than swapped out every quarter.
3. **Measurable recognition:** treated as a candidate asset until category-buyer surveys confirm high fame and high uniqueness via `asset_grid.py`.

Guardrail: never invent trademark clearance, survey percentages, or historical origin stories. Any proposed character, visual motif, or spoken tagline stays marked as a candidate to test with real buyers first.
