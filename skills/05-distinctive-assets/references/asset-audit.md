# Distinctive asset audit

## What counts as an asset

Any sensory cue that can trigger the brand without the name: logo shape, colour or colour pair, typeface, pattern, packaging shape, tagline or recurring line, character or mascot, celebrity or founder, sound, jingle or sonic logo, spoken intro, a recurring format (a weekly email with the same subject line). The brand name itself is the anchor, not an asset to score.

## Measuring

The standard measure is a prompted recognition check among **category buyers** (people who buy in the category, not only the brand's own fans or clients):
1. Show (or play) the asset with the brand name removed.
2. Ask an open question: *Which company is this?* No list of options.
3. Code the answers: our brand, another named brand, don't know.
4. Fame = our brand / everyone asked. Uniqueness = our brand / (our brand + another brand).

Sample notes to state every time: who was asked, how they were reached, how many, and any bias (a newsletter audience that has seen the brand's sponsorship will over-recognise it). Very small attribution counts (under about 10) make uniqueness unreliable. Repeat the same check, with the same method, to track progress.

## The grid (Romaniuk)

| | Uniqueness high | Uniqueness low |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fame high** | Use or lose: the brand's strongest cues; use everywhere, never drop casually | Avoid solo use: buyers know it but share it with others; always pair with the name |
| **Fame low** | Investment potential: unique but not yet known; use consistently to build fame | Ignore or test: neither known nor unique; drop or test before investing |

The 50% lines are a working heuristic from Romaniuk's practice, not a law. Report the numbers beside the label.

## Decision rules

- Few, consistent, for years. A small core set used together beats many assets used occasionally.
- Fame is built by consistent use in front of category buyers, not by redesign. Every change resets some of it.
- A generic category phrase or default colour cannot become distinctive, however good it sounds.
- Inconsistency (two colours, three taglines) splits fame; pick one and retire the others.
- New assets go through the same test before money is spent on them.
- Trademark and registration status: unknown unless documented. Recommend a clearance check by a qualified professional before investing in any new name, line or mark.

## Physical and mental availability

Assets serve mental availability (being thought of and recognised). Placing them where buyers already look (proposals, recurring emails, podcast intros, the website header) multiplies exposure at little cost. Sharp's work on category buyers is from consumer markets; apply it to niche B2B with that caveat stated.
