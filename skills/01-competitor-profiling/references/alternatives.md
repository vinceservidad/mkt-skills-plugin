# Building the alternative set

## The four groups

| Group | What it is | Typical evidence that buyers weigh it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct | Same kind of solution, same buyer (another specialist provider, another subscription) | lost deals to them by name, buyers comparing you in calls, their ads on your search terms |
| Indirect | A different way to do the same job (software instead of a service, a fractional executive instead of a provider, a marketplace) | lost-deal reasons, "we went with X instead", reviews mentioning them |
| DIY or in-house | The owner, an employee, a part-time hire, a spreadsheet, a template | interviews about what they did before, "not ready" losses, support tickets |
| Status quo | Doing nothing, waiting, relying on someone who already serves them for another job | "not ready" or "no decision" losses, long sales cycles, churn back to nothing |

The status quo is usually the biggest competitor and the one most often left off. Include it whenever lost deals, churn or interviews show buyers stopping or waiting.

## What to capture for each alternative

| Field | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Promise | As they state it, copied exactly if quoted, with where and when it was seen |
| Price | Observed price and date, or *not shown*. Never estimate a competitor's price from memory |
| Proof shown | Their reviews, case studies and counts are *claimed by the company*; say where the number appears |
| Wins when | From buyer evidence: why buyers chose it (lost-deal reasons, interviews, reviews) |
| Loses when | From buyer evidence: why buyers left it or rejected it |
| Evidence | Kind (observed, reported, inferred, unknown), source and date |

## Evidence kinds

- **Observed:** you or the user saw it on a dated page, pricing page, ad, ad library or search result. Record the date. Pages change; an observation is true only for its date.
- **Reported:** a buyer, rep or the user said it ("they promised strategy").
- **Inferred:** your reading of the evidence. Mark `(assumption)`.
- **Unknown:** say what would fill it: a mystery-shop call, a win/loss interview, a pricing-page check.

## Traps

- Copying a competitor because it is visible. A long-running ad or a busy pricing page shows what they do, not that it works.
- Ad library or search observations stated as spend, reach or performance. Presence is not spend; if no spend figures were shown, spend is unknown.
- Their review counts and customer counts repeated as facts. They are the competitor's claims.
- A "weakness" that is only our opinion. Weaknesses come from buyers' reasons for leaving or rejecting it.
- A list of famous brands in the category with no evidence that our buyers weigh them.
- Current platform facts (what an ad library or search engine shows, how it ranks) stated without first-party verification. Describe only what was observed and when.
