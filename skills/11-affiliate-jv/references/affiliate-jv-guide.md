# Affiliate and JV guide

How to set the economics, choose partners, track referrals and write the terms. Classify what you write: the commission formula below is a calculation from the business's own numbers; any "typical rate" is a heuristic from someone else's business and never evidence here.

## 1. The payable outcome

Pay for the outcome the business is paid for.

- **Service or subscription:** a client who signs and is still paying after the refund or trial window (for example, after the first paid month or second box).
- **One-off product:** an order that is not refunded or charged back within the stated period.
- **Qualification:** the referred buyer meets the ICP; a disqualified segment (too small, wrong country, wrong need) earns nothing or a lower fixed fee the owner sets.
- **Clawback:** a refund, cancellation or chargeback inside the period reverses the commission.

Clicks, leads and sign-ups are signals. Paying on them invites low-quality volume unless the owner chooses a fixed lead fee with a qualification rule.

## 2. Economics: what one payable sale can carry

Write every step with its source and label (*observed*, *owner's estimate*, *calculated*):

1. Monthly (or per-order) revenue per customer, from Price and model or the sales notes.
2. Gross margin %, from the owner's numbers. If unknown, stop the number here: the commission is *to set by the owner*, and the plan writes the formula.
3. Expected paid months in year one: the lesser of 12 and the observed retention (median tenure or a cohort curve). Use the observed figure, not a hope.
4. First-year gross margin = revenue × margin % × paid months (calculated).
5. Commission ceiling = the share of first-year gross margin the owner is willing to give up to acquire a customer. Show what a partner's ask equals as a share of that margin, for example: 30% of first-year fees ÷ (margin % × first-year fees).
6. Compare with the business's other acquisition costs where they are recorded; never with an industry average.

Recurring commissions (a share of every month) suit subscription partners who keep sending; one-off bounties suit introductions. JV splits on a launch are a share of launch revenue the partner's list produced, settled after refunds.

## 3. Partner types and how to judge them

| Type | What they bring | Judge on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing customers | Warm introductions | Route to `16-referral-program`; they are not affiliates |
| Complementary service providers (accountants, lawyers, agencies, consultants) | Introductions at the moment of need | Audience fit; whether their profession limits referral fees |
| Communities, coaches, educators | Access to a membership | The real make-up of the membership against the ICP; the figure is their claim until shown |
| Content creators, newsletters, podcasts | Reach to readers or listeners | Audience fit; one creator's deal is `11-influencer` |
| Coupon, cashback and deal sites | Buyers already at checkout | Usually intercept existing buyers; lower rate or excluded |
| Affiliate networks | Many small publishers | Fees, fraud controls, network rules; cite current documentation |

A partner whose audience is mostly a disqualified segment gets a qualification rule (pay only on qualified clients) or a polite no.

## 4. Tracking and attribution

- One unique link per partner; a code only if the owner approves a discount.
- Record the partner on the lead and the customer in the CRM, not only in a "how did you hear" field.
- Define the attribution window and the rule when two partners claim the same buyer (first introduction, last click, or the owner decides).
- Last-click credit shows who touched the sale, not whether the partner caused it. Whether the program adds sales the business would not have had is a question for `17-incrementality`.

## 5. Policy and fraud screens

- No bidding on the brand name or close variants unless the owner allows it.
- Coupon and cashback sites: allowed or not, and at what rate.
- No self-referrals, no paid or incentivised clicks, no cookie stuffing; review sudden spikes before paying.
- Claims rules: partners use only the approved offer, prices and proof.

## 6. Terms to settle in writing

Payable event and qualification; rate or split; attribution window; clawback period; payment timing and minimum; caps tied to capacity; brand, claims and disclosure rules; data and privacy; termination. Disclosure of affiliate links and paid endorsements is required in many jurisdictions, and some regulated professions (for example accountants, lawyers, financial advisers, health professionals) restrict or prohibit referral fees. Verify in current official guidance for the jurisdiction or with qualified advice, or mark *to confirm*. The plan is not legal advice.

## 7. JV launches

A JV partner mails their list about a launch for a split of the revenue it produces. Settle the dates, the offer and capacity with the owner (`12-launch`), give the partner a unique link, pay after the refund window, and cap the number of buyers to what the business can serve.
