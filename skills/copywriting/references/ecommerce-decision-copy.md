# Ecommerce decision copy and local meaning

```yaml
artifact_type: playbook
decision: write the page information needed for the reader's actual next decision
scope: product, collection, landing, policy and lifecycle wording; paid ads and page diagnosis retain their owners
owner: copywriting
inputs: product truth, reader situation, upstream promise, approved commercial terms, research and brand voice
evidence_status: inferred
confidence: not-assessed
freshness: 180-days
dependencies: customer-research provenance, supplied CRO brief, approved offer and policy terms
authorization: draft
rollback_or_stop: remove invented benefits or obligations; flag missing local or product evidence before publication
```

## Match the piece to the decision

Use the actual query, ad, email or customer question to determine what the page must clarify. Write the requested copy first, then concise evidence notes. A framework name, wireframe or messaging hierarchy alone does not complete a copy request.

| Reader job | Useful wording to deliver | Required source check |
|---|---|---|
| Choose a product or variant | Explain practical differences, fit, compatibility and how to select | Actual assortment and variant facts; no invented universal suitability |
| Decide whether the product fits their situation | Connect a verified feature to a supportable use or benefit and available proof | Benefit scope, genuine demonstration or research; evocative imagery is not proof |
| Understand the cost or commitment | State price basis, shipping, taxes/duties where supplied, eligibility and material offer terms | Approved commercial terms; a free item can still have delivery charges or obligations |
| Understand how purchase or use works | Give a clear next step, sequence and relevant effort/time limits | Actual order/use process; no fictional ease or arrival promise |
| Resolve a policy concern | Write plain returns, cancellation, warranty or contact information | Real policy scope and exceptions, retained beside the assurance |

Continue the upstream promise at the destination. Do not use a broad collection as a substitute for the exact product, eligibility or price promised in the ad. Route a suspected conversion gap to CRO; a copy revision is draft work, not proof of a lift.

## Make voice concrete

Translate supplied voice traits into short approved and rejected examples for the actual surface. A trait such as friendly does not authorize slang, invented familiarity or unsupported claims. Adjust tone for purchase questions, delivery problems or complaints while preserving the factual promise. Without supplied guidelines, label the voice a best-effort draft.

Use traceable customer research for language and objections; separate a quote from a paraphrase or an illustrative scenario. A remembered testimonial, another brand's reviews or an imagined customer story cannot become this business's evidence. Use facts that answer the decision rather than insisting that benefits, emotion or storytelling always beat technical information.

Apply [Drafting and editing](drafting-and-editing.md). Remove stock claims and corporate filler, vary sentence rhythm where useful, and preserve necessary context. Shorter is not automatically better when it removes compatibility, costs, policy conditions or a meaningful explanation. A piece can have several supporting links serving the same job; do not impose one button or one profession as a universal rule.

## Make the information order deliberate

When information order is part of the request, propose a clear opening, supporting explanation and next action from the reader's decision. Beginning/end emphasis is an ordering hypothesis, not a conversion law; keep qualifications beside their claims regardless of position. State what comprehension or recall question the proposed order could answer and hand the actual experiment to CRO/Tracking rather than designing a test or promising purchases here. Do not impose the same hierarchy on every page or menu.

## Localize meaning and the destination

Check terminology, units, currency, price basis, product availability, shipping/return terms and the destination together. A literal translation can change a promise or leave an unavailable offer in place. Use a competent local review where meaning, cultural context or a regulated claim is unresolved; do not claim that a translated draft is locally verified. Copywriting changes the words, not the approved price, product, policy or country eligibility.
