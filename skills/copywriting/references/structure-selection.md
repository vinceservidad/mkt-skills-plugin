# Intent-Led Structure Selection

A structure arranges an argument. It does not supply customer insight, proof or a performance result. Choose the reader's decision before choosing a named pattern.

## Selection sequence

1. **Reader's task:** What should this piece help the reader understand, compare, resolve or do? Name the next action, not just a funnel label.
2. **Available evidence:** Identify product facts, approved offer terms, demonstrations, comparisons and traceable customer language. Keep missing proof missing.
3. **Friction:** Name the evidenced question or objection preventing that action. Do not invent fear, urgency or audience psychology.
4. **Audience state:** Use supported awareness and belief information. Unknown awareness is not automatically low awareness. Ask only when the missing information would change the draft; otherwise use factual direct copy and state the assumption separately.
5. **Format and destination:** Match page section, email, length, reading context and CTA to the actual destination. Channel owners govern current platform requirements; lifecycle marketing governs sequence logic.
6. **Smallest useful argument:** Select the arrangement that explains the decision with the least unnecessary setup. A direct explanation may be better than a named persuasion pattern.

## Patterns and evidence requirements

| Arrangement | Reader decision | Evidence needed | Suitable use | Avoid when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Direct clarity | Understand a fact or complete an action | Exact task, conditions and next step | Setup, microcopy, transactional text, concise product explanation | The reader still needs a substantive objection or comparison addressed |
| AIDA | Understand an unfamiliar offer and decide whether to investigate | Recognizable situation, product facts and supportable reasons to care | Introductions, landing-page sections, prospecting email | Attention or desire would require manufactured drama; the reader already needs a direct answer |
| PAS | Resolve a recognized problem | Evidence of the problem and its consequences; a supported solution | Problem-led sections or messages | The problem is assumed, consequences are exaggerated, or agitation adds pressure without information |
| FAB | Evaluate a capability and its practical relevance | Real feature, mechanism and justified advantage/benefit | Product explanation, comparison, objections, sales email | The claimed benefit exceeds the feature or proof; a feature inventory obscures the decision |
| BAB | Understand an evidenced change and the path between states | A truthful before/after demonstration with scope and qualifiers | Case examples, process explanations, supported transformation stories | The after-state is invented, unverified or presented as a guaranteed outcome |

Awareness informs fit; it does not dictate a formula. Two pieces for the same awareness stage may need different arrangements because their decisions, evidence, objections or formats differ. Some sources order feature/benefit/advantage differently; the important requirement is a coherent, supported connection, not allegiance to an acronym.

## Narrative is a separate choice

A customer or creator narrative is a delivery format, not an awareness-independent persuasion rule. An owned-page or email story still needs a relevant reader decision and genuine, permitted source experience. Never manufacture first-person testimony, customer quotations or authenticity. Paid-ad narratives, hooks and creative concepts stay with `$creative-strategy`; current channel fit stays with the channel owner.

## Rule of One at the piece level

Keep one primary reader decision, promise and next action per piece. A homepage may serve marketers, agencies and founders when they share that job. Supporting audience examples may differ without requiring three competing promises or primary CTAs. When roles have conflicting needs, name the primary decision and separate their supporting sections; do not silently treat everyone as the same buyer.

## Deliverable

Write the requested headline, body, CTA or other artifact first. Keep framework headings out of consumer-facing copy. Add a short note afterward only when useful: the communication job, selected arrangement and reason, source/assumption limits, and what needs verification. A structure choice is not proof that the draft will convert.
