# Drafting and Editing

Use after the reader's task, facts, commercial terms and requested artifact are clear. This is an editing method, not evidence of conversion lift or a certified brand voice.

## Produce the requested work

- **Page:** Return the requested headline, supporting copy, body, CTA and microcopy in placement order. Keep approved offer conditions near the claim they qualify.
- **Email:** Return subject, preheader when requested, body and CTA. Follow the supplied lifecycle/sequence brief rather than inventing triggers, timing, consent or segmentation.
- **Short rewrite:** Rewrite the supplied text. Do not expand a sentence request into a strategy report or creative matrix.
- **Paid creative:** `$creative-strategy` owns the angle, hook and paid copy. It may reuse this editing pass without transferring ownership. The body must deliver the hook's promise and lead to the real destination.

Missing customer quotations do not prevent factual product copy. Do not invent VOC to fill the gap. Missing material product facts or offer terms may require one focused question or a clearly marked placeholder; never label incomplete placeholder copy ready to publish.

## Editing pass

1. **Intent:** Can the reader tell what the offer or instruction is, why the supplied facts matter, and what happens next?
2. **Facts:** Check every explicit and implied claim, number, comparison, policy, deadline and qualification against the supplied source. Preserve exact prices, windows and conditions.
3. **Specificity:** Prefer concrete nouns and verbs to abstract promises. Replace an inflated benefit with the supported mechanism or fact; do not merely substitute another adjective.
4. **Economy:** Cut stock openings, framework announcements, mechanical transitions, redundant summaries and unnecessary repeated contrasts. Keep information needed for a decision.
5. **Rhythm and voice:** Read the piece aloud. Vary sentence length where it improves meaning; do not force every sentence short. Use supplied voice guidance and traceable language, without impersonating a customer or copying distinctive third-party wording.
6. **Continuity:** The headline/hook, body, offer and CTA must agree. Deliver the promised point; do not earn attention for a proposition the destination cannot support.
7. **Action:** Use one primary next step appropriate to the reader's decision. A CTA states the action; it does not invent urgency or guaranteed results.
8. **Final check:** Remove unsupported intensifiers and vague rhetoric while keeping necessary qualifications. Judge words in context, not through a universal banned-word list.

## Presentation and proof

Put the usable draft first, without AIDA/PAS scaffolding in the copy. Follow with concise notes on source limits, assumptions, status and verification where needed. Keep research ledgers and framework analysis out of consumer-facing prose. State draft status; do not call the copy a winner, publish it, change an offer or launch ads through this skill.

## Quality check

A draft is acceptable only if it answers the brief, fits the reader's next decision, preserves facts and commercial terms, makes the next action clear, and reads naturally for the supplied context. Correct structure names and passing software checks do not establish writing quality. Review the actual draft.
