# PR guide

## Story types

| Type | Needs | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founder story | The Story section; the founder's own facts | Other people in the story (family, first customers) need permission to be named or described |
| Data story | Data the business owns, how it was pulled, who it covers | A business's customers are not the industry; customer agreements may bar publishing even totals |
| Contrarian view | The Big idea or Enemy, with evidence | Must be a view the founder will defend in an interview |
| Customer result | A named or described customer, with permission | Results need the customer's sign-off and a source |
| Newsjacking | A real, dated news event and something useful to add | Never a tragedy; never an invented event |

## Outlet fit

Rank outlets by evidence that the ICP reads, listens to or watches them (discovery calls, interviews, surveys, the owner's own reading of buyer behaviour), then by reachability (a host who already knows the founder, a newsletter that takes guest pieces, a local outlet with a business beat). A large national outlet is usually a stretch goal: write the story that could earn it, but do not make it the plan without evidence the buyer reads it and a route to reach it.

## The pitch

- Subject: the story in a few words, not "Press release" or "Collaboration".
- First lines: why it matters to this outlet's readers now.
- Evidence: one or two facts with their source.
- Offer: what the founder can give (an interview, the data, a written piece, a guest slot).
- Close: a short question. One follow-up after several days, then stop.

No attachments by default, no embargo games, no exclusives promised to more than one outlet.

## Data stories

State the base in the story: "Among our 24 client agencies in 2025" is honest; "Agencies wait 41 days to get paid" is not. Give the median and the share with how they were computed. Before publishing, check the customer agreement and ask the owner whether customers must consent to anonymised totals; if unclear, the story is blocked until permission. Flag for legal review where data is confidential.

## Newsjacking rules

- The news is real and dated, with a URL and date or a dated note in `inputs/`.
- The business adds information or a view, not just its name.
- No disasters, deaths, crimes or anyone's harm as a hook.
- If no current event fits, write the rule (what kind of news, what the business would add) and leave the hook empty.

## Tracking

Log each pitch and each piece of coverage. Judge coverage by leads or calls that cite it (CRM source field, a dedicated link) with counts. Note that self-reported source fields under-count and coverage is not proof of incrementality.
