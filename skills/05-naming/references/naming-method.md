# Naming method

## Brand architecture (Aaker)

| Architecture | Shape | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Branded house | Master brand + descriptor (*Brand Advisory*) | Same audience and promise; the master brand's trust should carry over. Cheapest to build. |
| Endorsed | Product name *by* or *from* Brand | A distinct offer that still benefits from the master brand's trust |
| House of brands | Standalone brand | A different audience, a reputational risk to keep apart, or a planned sale. Most expensive: the new name starts with no fame. |

Features and plans: descriptive names under the master brand by default (*Monthly close*, *Starter plan*). Name a feature with a coined name only when it is a reason to buy on its own and will be marketed.

## Name types

| Type | Example shape | Strength | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Descriptive | says what it does | instantly clear | generic, hard to protect |
| Evocative | suggests a benefit or feeling | memorable, ownable | needs explaining at first |
| Invented | a coined word | most protectable | no meaning until built |
| Founder or place | a person or location | authentic | hard to extend, key-person tie |
| Compound | two words joined | distinctive, can be clear | can be clumsy |

## Neumeier's seven criteria (The Brand Gap)

1. **Distinctiveness:** stands apart from the alternatives' names (judge only from dated evidence; a phrase common in the category fails).
2. **Brevity:** short enough to say and remember; resists nicknames.
3. **Appropriateness:** fits what it names and who it is for, without boxing in the future.
4. **Easy spelling and pronunciation:** heard once, spelled right; read once, said right.
5. **Likability:** people enjoy saying it (a test with buyers is better than a team vote).
6. **Extendibility:** room for future products, plans or markets.
7. **Protectability:** can be trademarked and owned; descriptive names are weakest here. Only a clearance search can confirm it.

Score each criterion strong, fair or weak, with a reason. The scores structure judgement; they don't prove a name will work.

## Availability checks (always listed, never assumed)

- Trademark clearance search in the relevant classes and countries, by a qualified attorney. A quick search by the team is a screen, not a clearance.
- Domain (exact and close variants).
- Social handles where the buyers are.
- Company registry, for a company name.
- Meaning and pronunciation in the languages of the markets served (ask native speakers).
Report each as *not done* unless a dated result is supplied.

## Buyer test

Show three to five finalists to five to ten target buyers, one at a time: *What do you think this is? Who is it for?* Then say each aloud and ask them to spell it. Look for names that are understood (or intriguing in the right way) and spelled right. This is qualitative screening, not proof of preference.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing by team vote on taste.
- A generic category phrase (it fails distinctiveness and protectability).
- A name that promises more than the offer delivers.
- Renaming a name that already has fame (check `brand.md` → Distinctive assets first).
- Calling a name available because a domain search looked clear.
