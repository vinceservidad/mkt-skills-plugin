# Search ad copy: query, promise and destination

```yaml
artifact_type: playbook
decision: produce usable paid Search ad assets from an evidenced query and approved offer
scope: Search copy and destination handoff; account configuration stays with google-ads
owner: creative-strategy
inputs: query or keyword themes, buyer decision, product truth, approved terms, evidence, final destination, locale
evidence_status: inferred
confidence: not-assessed
freshness: 180-days
dependencies: copywriting editing pass, google-ads platform validation, supplied product and offer evidence
authorization: draft
rollback_or_stop: remove unsupported promises; hold insertion or assembly that cannot preserve required meaning
```

## When to use

Use when asked to write or revise paid Search headlines, descriptions or an ad-to-page message handoff. Return the requested drafts first. Do not turn a short rewrite into a campaign audit, insist on a funnel model, or ask for an entire creative matrix when the supplied facts support the bounded task.

## Draft the actual ad

Read the supplied queries as buying situations, not just strings to repeat. Distinguish a product search, comparison, compatibility question, delivery need and price-sensitive request when the evidence supports that distinction. A keyword is a targeting input; a search term is the user's actual query. Do not infer either from an imagined persona.

For each genuinely distinct intent, identify the decision, relevant product or collection, supportable promise, proof and destination. Write headlines that perform different useful jobs: identify the product, explain a verified benefit, answer a relevant objection, express approved terms, or invite the next action. Cosmetic synonyms are not new strategic coverage. A feature can be the decisive information; do not replace compatibility, material, dimensions or eligibility with an unsupported emotional benefit.

Descriptions should complete the useful information rather than restate every headline. Make the CTA accurately describe the destination. A comparison query does not justify a false competitor affiliation; a delivery query does not justify invented stock, arrival dates or free shipping. A request for cheap products does not itself justify a negative keyword or a new discount.

Respect a requested quantity and format. For an unspecified single English-language RSA draft, offer six distinct headlines and two descriptions as an editable working set, not a platform requirement or effectiveness formula. Supply fewer supported variants with an explicit gap when the brief cannot support meaningful breadth. The Google Ads owner verifies minimum/maximum asset counts, character accounting, current serving behavior and locale constraints before platform use.

## Keep meaning intact when assets combine

Read every asset on its own and in plausible reordered, shortened or omitted combinations. Required offer qualifications must survive. Prefer a self-contained qualified claim over a headline whose truth depends on an optional companion. Mark any mandatory text and the reason it must remain visible; have Google Ads assess current pinning and automated-text behavior. Do not silently pin, enable automation or change an account to make the draft fit.

For insertion, review the actual eligible keyword set and fallback text with Google Ads. The inserted keyword must fit the sentence, length, product availability and evidence; it is not necessarily the exact search term. Leave insertion out when its alternatives would imply unsupported inventory, an affiliation or a different offer. A brand-safe fallback does not make unsafe inserted variants acceptable.

Use [Ad evidence and assembled copy](ad-evidence-and-assembly.md) when public references, testimonials or modular/catalog assets are involved, and [Drafting and editing](../../copywriting/references/drafting-and-editing.md) for the language pass.

## Output and handoff

Return the requested assets in placement order, with character counts where useful, the supplied final URL and optional supported display paths. After the copy, give concise notes on intent, claim sources, exact offer terms, mandatory qualifications, insertion/pinning questions and draft status. Never invent a final URL or label a draft published.

When page work is requested, hand Copywriting the query/ad promise, approved terms, destination, reader's unresolved question, proof and intended next action. Include the actual requested page wording with that owner; a handoff checklist is not a substitute for the deliverable. CRO owns page diagnosis and tests, Tracking owns validity, and Optimization Scaling owns expansion of an existing system.
