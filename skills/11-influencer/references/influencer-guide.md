# Influencer: decision rules

## Vetting tests (fit before reach)

A creator passes when all of these hold, each with its evidence:

1. **Audience is the buyer.** The niche, the comment questions and the location share match the ICP. Comments asking the questions the buyer asks (which beans, how to grind, how to close the books) are stronger evidence than follower counts.
2. **Not a disqualified segment.** If the ICP disqualifies a group (experienced enthusiasts, studios under 8 staff, non-US buyers), a creator whose audience is mostly that group fails, however large.
3. **Would use the product as it is.** A creator who reviews $1,000 machines, or mocks the buyer's current alternative, will not carry an offer built for beginners without sounding false.
4. **No conflict.** Recent sponsorships of a direct competitor, a stated refusal of products like this one, or an exclusivity elsewhere: ask before going further.
5. **Numbers hold up.** Views relative to followers, comment quality and growth history look like a real audience. Red flags: a sudden follower jump, views far below the follower count, comments that are generic or off-topic. Ask for the platform's own analytics (screenshots of audience location, age and recent reach) before any fee.

Label every number: *claimed* (media kit, the creator's message), *verified* (platform analytics the creator shared, with date), *calculated* (from the shortlist with `sum_by.py`). A ratio built on claimed numbers is still a claim.

## Deal types

| Deal | Use when | The business gets | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gifted seeding | Testing fit with small, high-fit creators | Product in the hands of people whose audience may care; no guaranteed post | Never imply a posting obligation after the gift; product and shipping cost are real spend |
| Paid sponsorship | A creator with proven fit and a deliverable you need | Agreed posts or videos by agreed dates | Fee against verified reach and fit, not follower count; approval rights; one test post before a series |
| Affiliate commission | The creator wants upside and the audience buys | Pay only on tracked sales | Commission is an owner commitment and its design belongs to `affiliate-jv`; last-click credit is not incrementality |
| Whitelisting / partnership ads | You want to run the creator's content or handle as ads | Ads that look like the creator's own posts | Explicit paid-usage rights, duration and spend caps; platform mechanics to verify; paid plan in `paid-social` |

Small high-fit creators first, as a test cohort, then expand on outcomes (heuristic).

## Budget and capacity

Show the arithmetic: fees + (boxes × product-and-shipping cost) ≤ the owner's budget; boxes ≤ stock or packing capacity. Keep a reserve for a second post from whoever works. An unknown product cost is an open question, not a number to guess.

## Brief

- Show the real product and its real terms (only offers, prices and claims in `offer.md` and `message.md`).
- Name the buyer's actual question the content should answer (from Voice of customer or tickets).
- Leave the voice and format to the creator; the audience trusts them, not the brand's script.
- Required: the disclosure the agreement sets; no health, income or result claims the business can't prove.

## Rights, disclosure and contract points

The agreement must settle, in writing, before anything is made:
- deliverables, platforms, dates and approval steps;
- exclusivity (competitors, period);
- usage rights: which content, which channels (organic reposts, website, email, paid ads), how long, whether whitelisting or partnership ads are included, and the fee for each;
- payment terms, and what happens if a post is not made;
- disclosure: the form required by the applicable law and the platform's branded-content tools.

Disclosure rules (for example consumer-protection endorsement guidance) and platform branded-content requirements are legal and platform facts: verify in current official guidance for the jurisdiction and platform, cite URL and date, or mark *to confirm*. Never advise hiding or softening a required disclosure. Flag the contract for qualified legal review; this skill identifies what the contract must address, it does not draft binding terms.

## Tracking and the read

- One unique link per creator (and a code only when the owner approves its terms). Record orders, subscribers or calls per creator.
- Judge each creator on cost per business outcome (fees + product cost ÷ outcomes), with the count. One or two orders are directional.
- Links and codes attribute; they do not prove the creator caused the sale. Causal claims go to `17-incrementality`.
- Content performance (views, saves, comments) is a signal and a guide to which content to reuse, not the decision.

## Platform claims

Branded-content labels, partnership-ad and whitelisting mechanics, creator-marketplace features and which audience insights a creator can share change. For each one the plan relies on: **documented** (the platform's own current documentation, `(first-party: <URL>, checked YYYY-MM-DD)`), or **to confirm**. No third-party benchmark is evidence about a creator.
