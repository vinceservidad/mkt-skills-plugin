# Value ladder guide

| Step | Job | Typical forms | Usual price relative to core | Moves up when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free | earn attention and permission; teach the problem | checklist, template, calculator, guide, quiz, community | $0 | they use it and hit the limit of doing it themselves |
| Low-ticket (tripwire) | turn a stranger into a customer; pay for acquisition | a small paid product, a catch-up or audit, a sample box | a small fraction of core | they get a quick win and want it ongoing |
| Core | deliver the dream outcome | the main service or product | — | they want more speed, depth or attention |
| High-ticket | serve the buyers who want more | done for you, advisory, premium tier, one-to-one | a multiple of core | — |
| Continuity | keep the relationship and the revenue | subscription, retainer, membership, annual plan | recurring | (it is the relationship) |

## Evidence that a step should exist

- **Free:** an asset already draws people, or buyers ask "how do I start?".
- **Low-ticket:**
  - a segment can't afford the core;
  - buyers want proof before committing;
  - an upfront problem, such as a catch-up, must be solved first.
- **High-ticket:** customers ask for more advice or attention, or say they would pay more for a specific deliverable.
- **Continuity:** the value recurs, and customers already buy repeatedly.

## Economics

- **Front end:** ideally covers the cost of acquiring the customer (self-liquidating).
- **Back end:** where the profit is: core retention, high-ticket offers, continuity.
- If the cost of acquisition, margin or repeat rates are unknown, say so and hand them to `17-unit-economics`.

## Common mistakes

- Steps that exist because a template says so, with no buyer asking for them.
- No trigger between steps, so nobody climbs.
- A low-ticket step that attracts buyers who never fit the core.
