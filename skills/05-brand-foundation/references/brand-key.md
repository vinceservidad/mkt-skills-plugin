# Brand key: slots, lenses and limits

The brand key (also called the brand pyramid) is a one-page brand definition. It is a framework: it structures the decision; it does not prove the brand will work. Every slot is filled from evidence or left as a named gap.

## The slots

| Slot | Question it answers | Where the evidence usually is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Insight | What tension in the buyer's life does the brand resolve? | Jobs to be done (struggling moment), pains, voice of customer |
| Purpose (why) | Why does the business exist beyond profit? | Founder notes, origin story. Never composed. |
| Root strength | What can we do that the alternatives can't? | Offer, proof stack, positioning differentiation |
| Functional benefits | What does the buyer get? | Core offer, dream outcome |
| Emotional benefits | How does the buyer feel? | Emotional jobs, desires, reviews |
| Values | What do we stand for, shown in behaviour? | Things the founder refuses, guarantees, practices |
| Personality and archetype | If the brand were a person, who? | Founder behaviour, what the buyer needs from a guide |
| Promise | What do we promise every time? | Dream outcome, guarantee, proof |
| Story | Who is the hero, who is the guide? | Founder story, message.md Story |
| Essence | The whole key in two to four words | Summary of the above (internal) |

## Golden Circle (Sinek)

Why → how → what. *Why* is the purpose, *how* the distinctive way of working (root strength, values), *what* the offer. Fill *why* only from the founder's own words or notes; if none exist, ask: *What made you start this? What do you refuse to do? What would customers lose if you closed?*

## Brand archetypes (Mark & Pearson)

Twelve personality patterns. Choose one primary; a secondary only if the evidence clearly shows it.

| Archetype | Core desire | Fits when the buyer needs… | Rules out |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Innocent | Safety, simplicity | reassurance, ease | edge, provocation |
| Sage | Truth, understanding | clarity, expertise, a straight answer | hype, playfulness as the lead |
| Explorer | Freedom, discovery | new experiences, independence | cosiness, rules-first tone |
| Outlaw | Disruption | permission to break with the status quo | conformity, corporate polish |
| Magician | Transformation | a change that feels remarkable | plodding detail as the lead |
| Hero | Mastery, courage | to win, to prove themselves | passivity, self-deprecation |
| Lover | Intimacy, pleasure | sensory delight, closeness | cold efficiency |
| Jester | Joy | fun, lightness | solemnity |
| Everyman | Belonging | to feel normal, unpretentious | elitism |
| Caregiver | To protect and help | to be looked after | detachment |
| Ruler | Control, order | stability, command | chaos, improvisation |
| Creator | Self-expression | to make something original | sameness |

Justify the choice from the buyer (what they need from the guide) and the founder's real behaviour. Say what the choice rules out in tone and imagery. The archetype is a lens for consistency, not evidence of preference.

## StoryBrand (Miller)

The customer is the hero, the brand is the guide. The guide shows empathy (we understand your problem) and authority (we've solved it). The structure: a character wants something, meets a problem (external, internal, philosophical), meets a guide who gives a plan and calls them to action, which helps them avoid failure and end in success. Use it to cast roles in the foundation; the full script is written by `08-story-epiphany-bridge` and `10-copywriting`.

## Keller's CBBE pyramid and Aaker's brand equity

Use them to diagnose what the brand lacks, not to fill the key:
- CBBE: salience (are we thought of?) → performance and imagery → judgments and feelings → resonance (loyalty, community). Most small brands fail at salience first.
- Brand equity model (Aaker): brand equity is built from four assets, awareness, associations, perceived quality and loyalty. Use it to say which asset a brand gap sits in, from evidence (recognition checks, reviews, repeat purchase), not to score the brand.
A gap at salience points to mental availability work; a gap at perceived quality points to product and proof.

## Mental and physical availability (Sharp, Romaniuk)

- **Mental availability:** the chance the brand comes to mind in a buying situation. Build it around **category entry points** (CEPs): the situations, needs and triggers in which buyers start looking (*when a client pays late*, *when a hire is on the table*). List CEPs from evidence: buying triggers, struggling moments, jobs.
- **Physical availability:** how easy the brand is to find and buy: search, referrals, directories, marketplaces, sales calls, booking.
- Sharp's finding: brands grow mainly by reaching more category buyers (penetration), not by deepening loyalty. Treat this as an empirical pattern from consumer categories, with exceptions; say so when applying it to a niche B2B service.

## The Long and the Short of It (Binet & Field)

From the IPA effectiveness database: brand-building works over long horizons and activation over short ones; across their cases, roughly 60% brand / 40% activation performed best, and the ratio varies by category and context (B2B and online-only brands differ). This is a pattern from a case database, not a rule. Never prescribe a ratio; name the data that would decide it and hand the split to `20-budget`.

## Share of voice (Jones; Binet & Field)

Excess share of voice (SOV above share of market) is associated with growth in the database studies. It needs measured data: category spend or mentions for us and competitors. Without it, write *share of voice: not measured*.

## Brand-building routes (options, not a plan)

- **Founder-led / personal brand:** the founder's own content and appearances. Strong when the founder is already visible and trusted; carries key-person risk.
- **Thought leadership:** publishing a distinct point of view the business can prove. Only claim expertise the evidence supports.
- **Sponsorships:** paying to be associated with an audience's event, newsletter or community. Judge by audience fit and measured response, not by impressions.
- **Referrals and word of mouth:** owned by `16-referral-community`.

Name each route only with evidence that it already works or fits (for example, lead sources), and hand the choice to `11-channel-selection`.
