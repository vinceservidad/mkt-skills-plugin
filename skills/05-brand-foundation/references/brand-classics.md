# Brand foundation classics

Use these proven filters when testing a brand's foundation on evidence. Never invent customer quotes, awards, or founding stories to satisfy a filter.

## 1. Purple Cow and Smallest Viable Market (Godin)

From Seth Godin (*Purple Cow*, *This Is Marketing*):

- **Remarkability sits inside the product, not the ad copy.** Ask what real capability, guarantee, turnaround, or experience in `offer.md` causes a satisfied buyer to tell a peer unprompted. If nothing in the offer is remarkable for the segment, flag the product/offer gap instead of pasting hype words onto the brand promise.
- **Smallest Viable Market:** identify the narrowest segment in `customer.md` whose problem, budget, and urgency match what the product already wins on. Build deep advocacy there before trying to speak to everyone.
- **Check against evidence:** confirm whether unprompted referrals, reviews, or repeat buyers actually cluster in that narrow group.

## 2. Brand Image and Long-Term Consistency (Ogilvy)

From David Ogilvy (*Ogilvy on Advertising*, *Confessions of an Advertising Man*):

- **Every touchpoint deposits into or withdraws from one brand image.** Check whether the tone, promise, proof, and offer conditions across ads, pages, and emails reinforce the same core personality.
- **Fact-grounded reputation beats clever stunts:** build authority from documented results, clear guarantees, and honest specifics. Short-term promotions that contradict the brand promise erode future conversion efficiency.

## 3. Values-Based Rally ("Think Different" Discipline, Jobs)

From Steve Jobs's 1997 "Think Different" approach at Apple:

- **Stand for a clear point of view when product specs look interchangeable.** Ask what the brand honors, builds for, or refuses to compromise on, proven by real operating choices and behaviors.
- **Proof test:** a brand belief only counts if real customer behavior, policies, or product design choices already demonstrate it in practice. Empty inspirational slogans go in questions for the owner, not in the foundation.

## 4. Sacrifice and Line Extension Trap (Ries & Trout)

From Al Ries and Jack Trout (*Positioning*, *The 22 Immutable Laws of Marketing*):

- **Law of Sacrifice:** choosing what the brand stands for requires giving up customers, price tiers, or features outside that focus.
- **Watch the Line Extension trap:** stretching one brand name across unrelated audiences or conflicting price tiers dilutes what the brand means in the buyer's mind. Flag competing promises inside the current product line for `06-positioning-statement` to resolve.
