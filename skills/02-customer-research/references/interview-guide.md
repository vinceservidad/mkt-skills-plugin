# Interview guide and research plan

## The Mom Test, in three rules (Rob Fitzpatrick)

1. Talk about their life, not your idea.
2. Ask about specifics in the past, not generics or opinions about the future.
3. Talk less and listen more.

Bad: "Would you use a service that…?", "How much would you pay?", "Do you think this is a good idea?"

Good: "When did this last happen?", "What did you do?", "What did that cost you?", "What have you tried?", "Why did you stop?"

## Who to recruit

| Group | Why | How many |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recent buyers (last 90 days) | they remember the trigger and the comparison | 5–8 |
| Churned or cancelled customers | they hold the real objections and failures | 3–5 |
| Lost deals or people who looked but didn't buy | they hold the reasons that block the sale | 3–5 |
| Non-customers with the problem | they show the status quo and DIY alternatives | 3–5 |

Five to eight interviews in one segment usually repeat themselves. Stop a segment when two interviews in a row add nothing new. For a new business with no customers, interview people who recently paid to solve the problem some other way.

## Screener

Use three to five questions that confirm the person has the problem and acted on it recently:
- "In the last [period], have you [done the job / paid for an alternative]?"
- "Who decided, and who paid?"
- "What did you use before that?"

Exclude friends, family and anyone who has never acted on the problem.

## Interview guide (30–40 minutes)

1. **Context.**
   - "Tell me about your [role/business/household]."
   - "Where does [the job] fit in your week?"
2. **The last time.**
   - "Walk me through the last time [problem] happened. When was it? What were you doing?"
3. **Trigger.**
   - "What made you start looking for something then, and not earlier?"
4. **Alternatives.**
   - "What did you try? What did it cost?"
   - "What was good about it?"
   - "Why did you stop?"
5. **Decision.**
   - "Who else was involved?"
   - "What did you compare?"
   - "What almost stopped you?"
   - "What did you need to see or hear?"
6. **Outcome.**
   - "What changed afterwards?"
   - "Has there been a week when it didn't happen, or you went without it? What happened?"
7. **Language.**
   - "How would you describe [the problem] to a friend in your situation?"
8. **Close.**
   - "Who else should I talk to?"
   - "Can I follow up?"

For churned customers, add:
- "What were you hoping it would do?"
- "When did you first think about leaving?"
- "What did you switch to?"

## Survey (only with 30+ customers or a list to send to)

- Keep it to 5–8 questions, mostly open-ended about past behaviour:
  - "What were you using before us?"
  - "What made you start looking?"
  - "What almost stopped you from buying?"
- Add one closed question to segment answers (role, size or use case).
- Do not ask for price willingness here. Pricing has its own skill and methods.
- Report response counts. Treat survey answers as stated preference, weaker than behaviour.

## Logging notes

Use one file per conversation, named `I-01`, `I-02` and so on. Each file holds:
- date and role;
- segment;
- how the person was recruited;
- verbatim lines in quotation marks;
- your observations marked as yours.

Remove names and anything identifying before the notes go into `inputs/`.

## Synthesis

- Tally each pain, desire, fear, trigger and alternative by interview ID.
- Keep a "surprises" list: anything that contradicts the founder's hypotheses goes at the top.
- Record single mentions as single mentions; do not round them up into a pattern.
