# Page structures

| Structure | Sequence | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PAS / PASTOR (Edwards) | Problem, Agitate, Solve / Problem, Amplify, Story, Transformation, Offer, Response | problem-aware readers |
| BAB | Before, After, Bridge | problem-aware readers; short sections |
| AIDA | Attention, Interest, Desire, Action | general pages |
| ACCA | Awareness, Comprehension, Conviction, Action | complex or B2B products |
| FAB | Features, Advantages, Benefits | product and feature pages for product-aware readers |
| StoryBrand BrandScript | Character, problem, guide, plan, call to action, failure, success | homepages |
| Problem–Promise–Proof–Proposal | the direct-response skeleton | landing and service pages |

## Applying the less obvious structures

- **Problem–Promise–Proof–Proposal:** name the problem in the buyer's words, state the promise, prove it from the proof stack, then make the proposal (the offer and one call to action). Use it for landing and service pages.
- **ACCA:** win awareness of the problem, build comprehension of what the offer is, create conviction with proof, then ask for the action. Use it where the reader must understand before they trust.

## Hero lead (Great Leads)

Choose the lead by awareness level (Masterson & Forde): offer, promise, problem–solution, big secret, proclamation or story. `10-headlines` holds the full lead table; in this skill, write the hero as the lead that fits the level.

## Homepage sections (typical)

1. Hero: promise and call to action.
2. Problem.
3. Guide: who we are and why we understand.
4. Plan: three steps.
5. Proof.
6. Offer summary.
7. Objections and FAQ.
8. Call to action.
9. Footer.

## Benefit ladder

Feature → what it does → what that means for them → why that matters. Stop at the rung that matches the job in `customer.md`.
