# Drivers, formats, and unconventional angles

## Emotional drivers

Use a driver only when the business's evidence shows it for this buyer. Cite the ID.

| Driver | The reason to care | Typical evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pain | What stops hurting | Pains, struggling moments, complaints |
| Desire | What they get to have or do | Dream outcome, desires, praise |
| Fear | The loss they avoid | Fears, scares, risk objections |
| Curiosity | Something they don't know yet and want to | A false belief, a surprising cause (the mechanism or the enemy) |
| Identity | People like me do this | Self-descriptions, segment language |
| Status | How it makes them look to others | Social jobs, peer comparisons |
| Enemy | The thing we both oppose | message.md → Enemy |
| Mechanism | Why this works when others didn't | message.md → Mechanism, Reason why |
| Proof | Who it already worked for | message.md → Proof stack (strong items only) |
| Price | Cost against the alternative or the cost of inaction | offer.md → Price and model; Current alternatives |

Price angles need the owner's real price. Proof angles need proof that exists and, for testimonials, permission.

## Awareness level → what the angle does

- **Unaware:** story, identity or a surprising fact about their world; no product first.
- **Problem aware:** name the problem in their words; agitate; hint at a way out.
- **Solution aware:** why this way beats the way they're considering: mechanism, comparison (only with evidence), proof.
- **Product aware:** answer the remaining doubt: proof, risk reversal, specifics.
- **Most aware:** the deal and the reason to act now (only true urgency).

## Formats

Static image · carousel or document · short vertical video · UGC or creator video · long-form video · native post (looks like the feed's organic content) · search ad · advertorial or native ad placement · email or newsletter sponsorship.

Format fit is a hypothesis. Current specs, placements and what a platform's delivery system favours change: cite the platform's own help pages with the URL and date checked, or write *to confirm*. Never state an undocumented algorithm preference.

## Guerrilla marketing

Low-cost, surprising stunts in physical or digital spaces. Include only when:
- the stunt carries an angle from the matrix (not attention for its own sake);
- it is legal where it runs (permits, property, trademarks) — mark *to confirm with counsel or venue*;
- the brand foundation and voice allow it;
- the result can be measured by a business outcome.

## Memes and culture-jacking

Riding a current meme, moment or trend. Include only when:
- the trend is observed and dated by the user or a supplied source; never assert from memory what is trending now;
- the image, audio or format can be used legally (rights to the image, sound or character) — otherwise *to confirm*;
- it is not a tragedy, crisis, protected group or political moment;
- the joke still carries the angle and the brand's voice, and the brand would be comfortable with it in a year.

A meme cell is a test like any other: hypothesis, outcome, stop rule.

## Picking first tests

Prefer cells that (1) sit at the level the awareness map says to win first, (2) rest on the strongest evidence, (3) differ from each other in driver, not wording. Placement crops and colour changes are adaptations, not new angles. A cell's win is evidence for that angle in that scope (audience, period, channel), not a universal rule.
