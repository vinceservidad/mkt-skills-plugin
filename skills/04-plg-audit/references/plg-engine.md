# The product-led engine: fit, links, habit and owners

Product-led growth (PLG) is a **strategy** choice: the product itself acquires, activates and converts users. Bush's MOAT questions and Eyal's Hook Model are **frameworks** for reasoning about it; neither proves that a product will grow. Funnel rates in a export are **observed** for that period and population only. Do not compare them with "typical" freemium or trial rates.

## 1. Fit: Bush's MOAT questions

| Question | Points toward product-led | Points away |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **M**arket strategy: dominant (better and cheaper for a broad market), differentiated (better for a niche), disruptive (simpler, cheaper for the underserved) | Dominant or disruptive strategies suit free or freemium; differentiated suits a free trial or demo | A niche, high-touch, high-price offer whose value needs explaining |
| **O**cean conditions: red (crowded, buyers know the category) or blue (new category) | Red oceans: buyers already know what to try, so self-serve trial works | Blue oceans need education first; demos or content lead |
| **A**udience: top-down (the executive buys) or bottom-up (the user adopts, then the team) | The user can start alone and see value without a purchase decision | The buyer never uses it, or legal, procurement or setup must happen first |
| **T**ime-to-value: can a user get value alone, quickly? | Value in minutes or the first session, with the user's own data | Value needs weeks of service, a specialist or a migration |

**Models.**
- *Freemium:* a free plan forever, paid for more (more use, more users, more features). Works when serving free users costs little and the free plan creates demand for the paid one.
- *Free trial:* full product for a limited time. Works when value is reached within the trial.
- *Free tool feeding a sales or service motion* (product-led sales): a free product that shows value and hands interested users to a call or a service. Common for service businesses; the paid step is a conversation, so the handoff matters as much as the product.
- *Not product-led:* when value needs a person, a long setup or a physical product. The nearest honest equivalent might be a sample, a paid trial, a demo or a lead magnet; those belong to `07-offer-design`, `07-pricing` (free trial vs freemium as a pricing choice) or `12-lead-magnet`.

## 2. The engine, link by link

| Link | Question | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signup | Who signs up, from where? | Segment captured at signup; source |
| Setup | Can they get the product working? | Connection or import failures are defects, not motivation |
| First meaningful value | Did they get the result they came for? | Not an automatic or system event; fix the denominator and window |
| Return (habit) | Do they come back without being chased? | Cohorts too young for the window |
| Paid step | Do the users who get value take the paid step? | Is the paid step visible at the moment of value, and affordable for them? |
| Expansion and referral | Do paying users add seats or bring others? | Invites, shares, word of mouth |

Read every link by cohort and segment. A blended rate across segments that behave differently hides the leak. A step can be *co-limiting* with another; say so rather than forcing one bottleneck.

## 3. First meaningful value

A candidate value event must be something the user experiences as the result (they saw next month's cash, they shipped the first invoice), and should be supported by evidence that it predicts return or payment. Events that are **not** value by default: account created, integration connected, a report generated automatically, a tutorial or checklist completed, an email opened. Record the candidate, the evidence for it (feedback, return rates of users who reached it vs not), and its strength (strong, moderate, weak, untested). Do not shorten necessary setup or qualification just to raise the rate.

## 4. Hook Model (Eyal)

1. **Trigger.** External (an email, a notification, a calendar moment) and internal (a feeling: worry about cash, boredom, uncertainty). PLG habit forms when the product answers the internal trigger.
2. **Action.** The simplest behaviour done in anticipation of a reward (open the forecast). Motivation × ability × trigger (Fogg).
3. **Variable reward.** What is new each time (this week's number, a change since last week). A static output gives no reason to return.
4. **Investment.** What the user puts in that makes the next cycle better (connected data, categories cleaned, history). Investment loads the next trigger.

Ask which link the evidence shows is weak: no return after a trigger means the trigger or reward fails; a low action rate means ability (effort) is the issue; low investment means the product does not get better with use.

## 5. Who it brings in

Compare the signup mix with the ICP and the owner's goal. If most signups are outside the segment that can buy the paid step, the engine is optimising the wrong audience: route acquisition sources to `11-channel-selection`, signup qualification to `13-signup`, and any idea of a separate paid plan for that segment to `07-pricing` and `07-value-ladder`. A user's statement of what they would pay is stated preference; it is input to pricing research, not a price.

## 6. Economics and measurement

- Cost to serve free users: infrastructure, per-account vendor fees, support time, from invoices or estimates (label estimates).
- Value of conversions: paid customers × their fees from the context, over a stated period; no lifetime value without a cohort.
- Attribution: how is a paid outcome from the product counted in the CRM? If product-sourced calls are recorded under another lead source, the product's contribution is inside those numbers, not on top. Instrumentation and definitions are a `17-tracking-plan` matter.
