# Finding a true unique mechanism

## The two halves

| Half | Question | Good evidence | Bad evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem mechanism | Why did what they tried before fail? | a buyer's account of what went wrong (*they categorised everything as software*), a lost or churned client's reason, a dated competitor claim that shows how they work | *people are lazy*; a cause that blames the buyer; a cause nobody reported |
| Solution mechanism | What, specifically, here deals with that cause? | the offer as delivered today; process notes; delivery documents | a feature marked proposed; a step the founder intends to add; a method from another company |

The mechanism is the bridge: *because [cause], earlier attempts failed; [part] deals with [cause], so [result].* Each link carries its proof or its gap.

## Tests

1. **Exists:** every part is delivered today. Proposed parts strengthen a future mechanism, not this one.
2. **Explains:** a buyer could repeat why it works in one sentence.
3. **Answers the cause:** each part maps to a cause the evidence shows. A part that answers no cause is a feature, not mechanism.
4. **Unique, honestly:** compared with the alternatives' dated claims. When competitors claim the same parts, the claimable uniqueness is the combination, order, cadence or customer, or nothing yet.
5. **Provable:** each link has proof or a named gap. Without proof the mechanism is a hypothesis, labelled as one.
6. **Fits the stage:** at stage 3 a first mechanism lands; at stage 4 it must be newer, easier or surer than mechanisms already claimed; at stage 5 identification may matter more.

## Naming

- Descriptive beats clever: the name should hint at how it works (*the weekly cash loop*, *the agency close*).
- The name may not promise more than the proof: a name with *Monday*, *48-hour* or *zero* must match what is delivered every time.
- Names are proposals: untested with buyers, not cleared for trademark. Do not write *proprietary*, *patented* or *™* unless a supplied document shows it.

## Traps

- **Invented ingredients.** Adding a step, test, algorithm or data source because a mechanism needs one.
- **Mechanism as causality.** *This is why clients grow* is a claim about results; it needs results evidence. Keep *how it works* separate from *what it achieved*.
- **Borrowed mechanism.** A competitor's visible mechanism is evidence about them, not something to copy or rename.
- **Science-washing.** Unnamed studies, *clinically proven*, *data shows* with no data.
- **Renaming a feature.** A brand name on an ordinary step is not a mechanism unless it explains why the result happens.
- **Rewriting the message.** The message skill owns how the mechanism is said in copy; this section owns what it is.
