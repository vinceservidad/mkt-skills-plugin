# Voice guide method

## Voice versus tone

- **Voice:** the constant personality. Comes from the brand foundation (archetype, personality, values).
- **Tone:** the adjustment to context and the reader's state. A reader who just got bad news needs a different tone from one browsing a homepage; the voice is still recognisably the same.

## From foundation to voice traits

Write three or four traits as pairs, *we are X, not Y*, where Y is the failure mode of X:

| Foundation signal | Possible trait |
| --- | --- |
| Sage archetype, value of honesty | *plain-spoken, not simplistic* |
| Caregiver archetype | *warm, not sugary* |
| Outlaw archetype | *bold, not rude* |
| Value of speed or precision | *specific, not vague* |
| Everyman archetype | *friendly, not overfamiliar* |

Each trait must trace to a foundation slot; drop traits that don't.

## The four dimensions (Nielsen Norman Group)

1. Funny ↔ serious
2. Formal ↔ casual
3. Respectful ↔ irreverent
4. Enthusiastic ↔ matter-of-fact

Place the voice on each, then show how tone moves along them by context (for example, more serious and more respectful in an apology than in a social post). NN/g's research found these dimensions affect perceived friendliness, trustworthiness and desirability; it does not prove any one position converts better for this business.

## Auditing current copy

Flag, with the exact line:
- **Jargon and filler:** *leverage, synergistic, best-in-class, holistic, empower, solutions, unlock potential*.
- **Hype without proof:** superlatives and claims no source supports (route to `08-proof-stack`).
- **Category clichés:** phrases competitors also use (only with a dated observation).
- **Passive excuses:** *due to circumstances beyond our control*; replace with ownership and a next step.
- **Over-enthusiasm:** stacked exclamation marks, *have a fantastic day!!* in a support reply.
Keep and model the lines that already work, often the founder's own writing.

## Tone by context (typical contexts)

| Context | Usual shift | Rule of thumb |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Website and sales pages | Most confident | Lead with the buyer's situation in their words |
| Sales and discovery emails | Direct, brief | One ask per email |
| Onboarding | Patient, step by step | Say what happens next and what we need |
| Support replies | Calm, specific | Answer first; name the person and the timing only if real |
| Bad news and apologies | Most serious and respectful | Own it, say what happened, what we're doing, what the client can expect; no excuses, no blame |
| Review replies | Respectful, brief | Thank, address the point, move details offline; never reveal client information |
| Social posts | Most casual | The founder's point of view, one idea per post |

## Words to use and avoid

- **Use:** the customer's own words from voice of customer and reviews, with IDs. These are proof the language resonates with buyers, not proof it converts.
- **Avoid:** each with a reason and a replacement.

## Rewrites

Show two to four real lines rewritten. A rewrite changes wording, rhythm and order; it adds no facts. If a natural rewrite needs a fact (a response time, a date, a name), write it as *(to confirm)* rather than inventing it.
