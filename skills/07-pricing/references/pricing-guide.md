# Pricing guide

## Four kinds of evidence (keep them apart)

| Kind | Examples | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observed behaviour | closed-deal values, conversion at a price, churn or downgrades by plan, upgrades, what customers paid alternatives | strongest |
| Stated preference | "I'd pay more for X", Van Westendorp or Gabor-Granger answers, survey ranges | useful for direction; people say and do different things |
| Competitor and alternative context | price lists, what a hire or a fractional executive costs | sets anchors and ranges; not proof of your price |
| Inference | your reasoning about value, cost or positioning | label it `(assumption)` |

## Models

| Model | Fits when | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Value-based flat fee | outcome value is clear and similar across buyers | big differences in buyer size |
| Tiered by size or value metric | value grows with the buyer (revenue band, seats, volume) | too many tiers; a metric buyers can't predict |
| Usage | value is tied to consumption | bill shock; hard to forecast |
| Subscription or continuity | value recurs (books every month, coffee every 2 weeks) | rigid schedules cause churn; give control (skip, pause) |
| One-time | a project or a catch-up | no recurring relationship unless a back end exists |
| Free trial | value is felt quickly | needs onboarding to reach value in the trial |
| Freemium | a large audience, low marginal cost, a clear upgrade trigger | free users who never pay |

## Anchoring and psychology, honestly

- **Anchor** against the alternative buyers already compare. For example, a fractional CFO at $5–6k a month against books at $1.5k.
- **Decoy:** a third option that makes the target option clearly better. Use it only if someone would genuinely buy it.
- **Charm pricing** ($19 vs $20) suits low-ticket consumer offers. Round numbers suit premium and professional services.
- **Payment plans** lower the effort term without changing the price.
- **Discounts** train buyers to wait. Prefer packaging, such as a lighter plan or an annual prepay, and discount only with a reason and an end date.

## Survey methods (stated preference)

- **Van Westendorp:** four questions (too cheap, cheap, expensive, too expensive). Report the acceptable range, not "the optimal price".
- **Gabor-Granger:** willingness to buy at set prices, giving a demand curve.

Report the sample size. Treat the results as stated preference.

## Before any live change

Before changing a live price, write down:
- the baseline;
- what will be measured: conversion, churn, revenue per customer, refunds;
- the window;
- the stopping rule;
- who approves.

This skill recommends; the owner decides.
