# Answer patterns by objection type

| Type | Belief to shift | Answer pattern | Typical proof |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Price or value | "It costs more than it's worth" | anchor against the cost of the problem or the real alternative; show the value gap; or offer a lighter step if the buyer is outside the core segment | a client's result in money; the anchor price, sourced |
| Vehicle or category | "This kind of solution can't do what I need" | show the mechanism doing the thing they think it can't, on their case | a sample or demo on their data; a case study |
| Trust or sameness | "They all promise the same" | specificity: what you do that others don't, with a number | logs, exact reviews, a named specialisation |
| Risk | "What if it fails or is late?" | point to the guarantee and the process that makes failure unlikely | the guarantee terms; an on-time record, sourced |
| Internal (control, ability) | "I'll lose control" or "I can't do it" | show what stays in their hands and how small the first step is | the process; a customer's words about control |
| Fit | "Not for someone like me" | either show that it is (a peer story) or agree and point to the right step | a peer case; a lighter plan (07-offer-design) |
| Timing | "Not now" | the cost of waiting; the trigger to watch for; a low-commitment next step | a peer's "I wish I'd started earlier", if it exists |

## Placement

- **Unaware and problem aware:** the enemy and the story address vehicle and internal beliefs.
- **Solution aware:** comparisons and demos address category and sameness objections.
- **Product and most aware:** the offer page, FAQ and call address price, risk, fit and timing.
- **After the sale:** onboarding answers "Did I make the right choice?"
