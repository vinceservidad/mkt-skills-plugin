# Visual execution classics (Reeves, Bernbach, Ogilvy)

Check every image prompt, shot list, and video storyboard against these three creative execution tests before finalizing direction. All visuals must respect truth rules: no fabricated screenshots, staged testimonials, or invented numbers.

## 1. Vampire Video check (Reeves)

From Rosser Reeves (*Reality in Advertising*): a dramatic, funny, or overly clever visual that pulls viewer memory away from the product and core selling claim is "vampire video". It sucks attention out of the message it was paid to deliver.

Pass/fail test for every storyboard and still:
- Ask: *if someone glances at this for 2 seconds and recalls the striking visual, do they also recall our brand cue and core selling claim?*
- Fix if it fails: simplify background clutter, center the product or customer moment carrying the claim, and keep on-screen text anchored directly to the single message.

## 2. Visual-verbal complement (Bernbach)

From Bill Bernbach: the strongest ads pair words and pictures so neither is redundant.
- Do not write headline text that merely captions what the photo already shows literally (*"Man smiling at laptop"* above a photo of a man smiling at a laptop).
- Instead, let the frame show the problem tension, real workspace artifact, or physical contrast while the headline names the shift or offer outcome, or show the proof visually while the headline speaks to the buyer's doubt.

## 3. Photo realism and caption rules (Ogilvy)

From David Ogilvy's direct-print and campaign research:
- **Real scenes beat abstract graphics** when selling tangible services or real products (use real photos or clearly labelled sample scenes; never stage fake customer results).
- **Story appeal over static poses:** frames showing action in progress or an intriguing moment hold attention longer than generic posed portraits.
- **Captions get read:** whenever a still asset includes a visible photo or sample artifact, add a concise factual caption carrying the key proof or explanation.
