# Proof classics (Bencivenga, Hopkins, Bernays)

Use these three proof methods when building and ranking `## Proof stack`. Never invent numbers, studies, client counts, or third-party endorsements; flag missing items clearly for collection.

## 1. Persuasion Equation (Bencivenga)

From Gary Bencivenga (*Bencivenga Bullets*), strong persuasion requires four linked pieces:

`Urgent Problem + Unique Promise + Unquestionable Proof + User-Friendly Proposition`

In `08-proof-stack`, verify whether the third link (**Unquestionable Proof**) is strong enough to carry the promise (`08-message-doc`) and offer (`07-offer-design`). Where buyers are skeptical (Sophistication Stages 3 to 5), rank **proof-before-promise** highest: lead the page or ad with undeniable evidence before asking buyers to believe the promise.

### Believability hierarchy (strongest to weakest)

1. Live demonstration or sample the buyer can inspect themselves (demo recording, audit sample, trial output).
2. Independent third-party data, press, or named, role-attributed customer results (with permission).
3. Verifiable mechanism proof (why the method works step by step, transparent process facts).
4. Conditional guarantee or risk reversal from `offer.md` (shows confidence with real terms).
5. Self-reported internal logs (valid only when explicitly labelled as internal logs with sample size and dates).

## 2. Preemptive claim (Hopkins)

From Claude Hopkins (*Scientific Advertising*, the Schlitz Beer lesson):

- **Claim an unpublicized true process competitors treat as ordinary.** Look inside the client's actual delivery SOPs, quality checks, materials, or turnaround controls in `inputs/`. If competitors all do or meet a standard quietly without telling buyers, telling the specific documented story first gives you ownership of that trust signal.
- **Replace vague boasts with exact documented facts:** never keep "fast", "pure", or "highest quality"; replace with the exact verified count, step, inspection, or measurement from `inputs/`, or soften the claim.

## 3. Third-party authority and opinion-leader proof (Bernays)

From Edward Bernays: buyers trust independent peers and respected third parties far more than self-praise. When auditing proof gaps, look for credible customer segments, industry associations, independent audits, or named peer testimonials (`16-testimonials-case-studies`, `11-pr`) and mark permission requirements explicitly before use.
