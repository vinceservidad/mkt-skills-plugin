# Persona template (empathy map)

Fill each field from evidence: interviews, calls, reviews, tickets. Where the evidence is silent, write "unknown", or mark an inference `(assumption)`. A persona with invented detail is worse than a short persona, because it gives later copy false confidence.

```markdown
Persona — <role label, e.g. "Studio owner" or "First-time subscriber">
Role in the purchase: finds / decides / pays / uses / influences
Situation: <when they hit the problem and what is going on around them>
Goals: <what they are trying to get done, in their terms>
Think and feel: <worries, hopes, pressures; quote where possible>
Say: "<verbatim>" — <source ID, date>; "<verbatim>" — <source ID, date>
Do: <what they currently do about it, tools and alternatives used>
Pains: <what goes wrong now>
Gains: <what better looks like, in their words>
Objections: <what makes them hesitate, with source>
Reached through: <where they already gather, with source or (assumption)>
```

## B2B or B2C

| | B2B | B2C |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ICP filters | industry, headcount, revenue band, revenue model, tools, growth stage, a recent event | situation, behaviour, current alternative, frequency, a recent event |
| Roles | often split: user, champion, economic buyer, finance, blockers | usually one person; sometimes a buyer for someone else (gift, parent) |
| Disqualifiers | too small to afford, wrong model, wants a different category of help | wrong use case, cannot get value (no equipment, no time), price-only shoppers |

## Rules

- No stock-photo detail. Leave out age, gender, hobbies, a made-up first name and a car, unless the evidence shows they change behaviour.
- One persona per role that behaves differently. Two personas that think the same are one.
- Keep a "not our customer" persona when churn or lost deals show a clear pattern. It is the disqualifier made concrete.
