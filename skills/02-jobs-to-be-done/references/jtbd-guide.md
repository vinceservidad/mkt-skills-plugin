# Jobs to be done: working guide

## Job statements

Form: **When** [situation], **I want to** [motivation], **so I can** [expected outcome].

| Weak | Why | Better |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "I want accounting software" | names a solution | "When a client pays late, I want to see whether payroll is safe, so I can decide without panicking" |
| "Busy moms want convenience" | a demographic and a vague benefit | "When I get home at 7 with nothing planned, I want dinner on the table in 10 minutes, so the evening isn't a fight" |
| "Users want a better experience" | no situation, no outcome | "When I start a new subscription, I want to know which bag suits my machine, so I don't waste $22 on beans I can't enjoy" |

## Forces of progress (Klement)

```
        PUSH of the situation          PULL of the new solution
   (what's wrong now: late books,   (what's attractive: weekly
    cash scares)                     forecast, agency expertise)
                     →  SWITCH  ←
   ANXIETY of the new solution     HABIT of the present
   (will they understand us?       ("my CPA handles it", the
    will I lose control?)           part-timer we already pay)
```

Ask, or look in the evidence, for each force:

- **Push:** "What was happening when you started looking?"
- **Pull:** "What did you hope would be different?"
- **Anxiety:** "What almost stopped you?" and "What worried you about switching?"
- **Habit:** "What did you like about the old way?" and "Why didn't you switch earlier?"

## Desired outcomes (Ulwick)

Form: [direction] + [metric] + [object of control] + [context].

Examples:
- "Minimise the time it takes to know last month's numbers."
- "Minimise the likelihood of running out of coffee before the next delivery."

Rank by:
- **Importance:** how often the outcome is mentioned, and how emotionally.
- **Satisfaction today:** how well current alternatives serve it.

An outcome that is important but poorly served is an opportunity.

## Before–After grid (DigitalMarketer)

| | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Have | what they have or lack | what they have now |
| Feel | emotion before | emotion after |
| Average day | what a day looks like with the problem | what it looks like without it |
| Status | how they are seen | how they are seen after |

Fill it with quotes where possible. The "after" column feeds the dream outcome in `07-offer` and the promise in `08-message`.

## Competing solutions

List everything hired for the job:
- direct competitors;
- adjacent categories;
- a person (hire, freelancer, family);
- a workaround (spreadsheet, supermarket beans);
- doing nothing.

When the main alternative is non-consumption, the message must sell the job before the product.
