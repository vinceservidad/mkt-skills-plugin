# Permission Marketing (Godin)

Use Seth Godin's *Permission Marketing* rules to evaluate how the email list grows, what emails earn attention, and why consent quality matters more than raw subscriber count. Keep all counts and opt-in wording tied to real exports and inputs.

## 1. The three tests of every email (Godin)

Every broadcast, newsletter, or re-permission send must earn its place in the inbox on three checks:

1. **Anticipated:** does the subscriber expect to hear from the sender on this topic and at this cadence based on the original sign-up wording?
2. **Personal:** is the email written directly to that segment's situation (`customers` vs. `non-customers`, by acquisition source) rather than blasting everyone identically?
3. **Relevant:** does it help solve a current problem in the buyer's journey (`03-journey-map`) rather than pushing an unrelated internal promo?

If a broadcast fails two or three of these, hold or segment it before raising send frequency.

## 2. Permission ladder (turn attention into trust)

Godin classifies audience relationships into five rungs. Plan `List growth` and `Broadcast calendar` around moving subscribers up one rung honestly:

| Level | State | Permitted action in email channel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Intravenous / Stranger | Never opted in or unclear source | **Do not mail.** Hold out of broadcasts; require clear opt-in before messaging. |
| 2. Situation permission | Joined for one lead magnet, order, or webinar | Deliver what was promised clearly; state what future emails will cover. |
| 3. Brand / Personal trust | Opens, reads, and stays subscribed over months | Maintain steady, predictable helpful cadence; introduce relevant core offers (`offer.md`). |
| 4. Relationship / Loyalty | Repeat buyer or active engaged customer | Tailor segments to usage/purchase history and gather honest feedback or re-engagement cleanly. |
| 5. Advocacy | Actively forwards, replies, and refers | Point satisfied readers to real referral or review channels (`16-referral-program`). |

## 3. Interruption vs. Permission check

Never fix falling clicks or revenue by buying third-party lists, scraping emails, hiding unsubscribe links, or doubling daily promos without owner approval and inbox-health evidence. Treating attention as an owned asset protects deliverability and long-term conversion.
