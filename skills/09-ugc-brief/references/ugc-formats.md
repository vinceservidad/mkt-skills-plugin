# UGC formats, brief anatomy, and authenticity rules

## Formats

| Format | Shape | Needs | Fits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem-solution | The problem in the creator's life → trying the product → what changed | A pain in VoC; the creator really has it | Problem and solution aware |
| Testimonial | Face to camera: before, what they tried, what it's like now | Real use for a stated period; the creator's own words | Solution and product aware |
| Unboxing | First open, what's inside, first impressions | The real package; nothing staged as "arrived" if it didn't | Product aware, gift buyers |
| Day-in-the-life | The product inside a normal day | A real routine where the product fits | Unaware and problem aware |
| Before-after | A typical before and after, with the time between | A real, typical result; no exaggerated contrast | Solution aware; never on unproven results |

## Brief anatomy

1. What this is for (one line, one outcome).
2. The moments that matter, from customers, with the reason and a question for the creator.
3. Optional hooks to try (multiple takes).
4. Must say (exact terms) and must not say (unproven, contradicted, regulated, competitor claims).
5. Disclosure.
6. Shot list and delivery (orientation, length and specs *to confirm*).
7. Terms set by the owner.

## Authenticity rules

- A creator says only what they experienced. If they haven't used it long enough to judge, they don't judge.
- Never give creators customer quotes to recite. Quotes in the brief show what buyers care about; the creator answers in their own words.
- No staged results, borrowed before-after images, or acted "customer" roles presented as real customers.
- Real customers filming testimonials need their consent for each use, and their personal details stay out.
- Paid or gifted content is disclosed. Laws (for example the US FTC's endorsement guidance) and platform branded-content rules set how; confirm the current wording and placement before publishing, and don't quote the rule from memory.

## Terms the owner sets

Payment or free product · usage rights (organic only, or paid ads; where; how long) · whitelisting or running ads from the creator's handle · exclusivity · number of revisions · delivery date. The skill never invents these.
