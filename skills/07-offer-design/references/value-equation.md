# Value Equation and Grand Slam Offer (Hormozi, $100M Offers)

```
Value = (Dream outcome × Perceived likelihood of achievement) ÷ (Time delay × Effort and sacrifice)
```

| Term | Raise it (or cut it) with | Evidence to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dream outcome ↑ | the buyer's own words for the result; status and emotion, not features | desires, jobs, after-state in the Before–After grid |
| Perceived likelihood ↑ | proof specific to them; a mechanism; a demonstration on their data; peer results; a guarantee | trust and sameness objections, questions asked on calls |
| Time delay ↓ | a fast first win; showing progress early | "how fast will I see…?" questions, churn timing |
| Effort and sacrifice ↓ | done for you; removing setup and paperwork; fewer decisions | onboarding complaints, ability objections, tickets |

## Grand Slam Offer steps

1. Identify the dream outcome.
2. List every problem the buyer will face before, during and after, including the ones that stop them from starting.
3. Turn each problem into a solution: "how to [solve problem] without [what they dislike]".
4. Choose delivery vehicles for each solution:
   - **Level of help:** do it yourself, done with you, done for you.
   - **Format:** one-to-one, group, software, a template or a call.
   - **Timing:** instant, on demand, scheduled.
5. Trim and stack. Keep what is high value to the buyer and low cost to you. Some items that are high in value and high in cost may stay as premium. Move the rest to bonuses or drop them.
6. Add the enhancers: name, bonuses, guarantee, scarcity and urgency. Each is owned by its own skill in this library.

## MAGIC naming

| Letter | Element | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M | Magnet: a reason to look now | "Free", "New", a season |
| A | Avatar: who it's for | "for agency owners" |
| G | Goal: the outcome | "know you can afford the next hire" |
| I | Interval: the time frame | "in five days", "every Monday" |
| C | Container: what it's called | "System", "Plan", "Club", "Box" |

Use three to five of the five elements. A name is a proposal until buyers have reacted to it.

## Trim rules

- Cut deliverables that no evidence says buyers want.
- Keep the deliverable that answers the most frequent objection, even if it is small.
- If the offer needs a new capability, mark it proposed. Do not present it as existing.
