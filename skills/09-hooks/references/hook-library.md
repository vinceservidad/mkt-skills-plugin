# Hook library

| Type | What it does | Needs from the evidence | Fits best |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Question | Asks the question already in the viewer's head | A real buyer question (calls, tickets, comments) | Problem and solution aware |
| Bold claim | States the result or the surprising truth plainly | Proof for the claim | Solution and product aware |
| Contrarian | Contradicts a belief the viewer holds | A documented false belief or enemy | Problem and solution aware |
| Curiosity gap | Opens a loop the body closes | A mechanism or cause they don't know | Unaware and problem aware |
| Pattern interrupt | An unexpected image, sound or action in frame one | A visual that carries the angle, not a random shock | Any; cold traffic |
| Callout | Names who it is for | A segment the ICP defines | Any; qualifies the viewer |
| Number | A specific count, time or amount | A real number from context | Solution and product aware |
| Story open | Drops into the middle of a moment | A real story (founder story, review, case) | Unaware and problem aware |

## The three layers of a video hook

- **Seen:** the first frame. What is on screen before anyone speaks.
- **Said:** the first spoken line.
- **On-screen text:** the words overlaid, readable with the sound off.

The three should carry one idea. When they say three different things, the viewer gets none.

## Honesty rules

- The payoff arrives quickly and keeps the hook's promise. A bait hook that the body doesn't pay off is a lie.
- A number hook uses a number from context, with its period and source.
- A story hook uses a real story. Customer stories need permission.
- A callout names a segment, not a personal attribute ("agency owners", not a statement about the viewer's health, money troubles or identity). Some platforms restrict personal-attribute language: check the current ad policy and mark *to confirm*.
- Contrarian hooks attack a belief or method, not a named competitor, unless there is a dated, sourced comparison.

## Testing hooks

Hold the body constant and vary only the hook, so the result can be attributed. Early-retention or hold metrics show whether the opening stopped people; the decision rests on the business outcome further down. A winning hook is evidence for that hook, audience and channel in that period, not a universal rule.
