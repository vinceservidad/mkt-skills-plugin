# Direct-mail & email playbook (Halbert)

Apply these Gary Halbert (*The Boron Letters*) principles when structuring single marketing emails, promos, and win-back letters. Every fact, price, deadline, and reason stays grounded in `context/`.

## 1. A-pile opener and greased slide (Halbert)

Halbert sorted incoming mail into two piles: **A-pile** (personal notes, trusted updates, real correspondence that gets opened immediately) and **B-pile** (obvious mass advertising that goes straight into the trash).

To keep marketing email feeling like A-pile mail:
- **Write human subjects and preheaders:** plain lower-to-sentence-case phrasing, honest specificity over brochure slogans, zero fake *Re:* or *Fwd:* tricks.
- **Skip the throat-clearing warm-up:** cut generic greetings (*"Hope this email finds you well"*) and open straight inside a moment, problem, or observation the segment recognizes from `Voice of customer`.
- **Greased slide opening:** start with a short, easy first sentence (five to twelve words) followed by crisp bridges that keep the reader sliding into the core point before pitching.

## 2. 80/20 research-before-writing discipline

Halbert spent roughly 80% of his time studying the market, offer, and buyer wording before typing a single word of copy. If `Beliefs, objections and drivers` or `Offer` terms are thin in context, stop and ask instead of guessing transitions.

## 3. Grabber and P.S. checklist

- **One clear letter voice:** write to one person (`you`), not a crowd (`everyone out there`).
- **One idea, one link/CTA:** avoid cluttering a broadcast email with three unrelated offers.
- **P.S. as a second hook:** restate the core benefit, real deadline (only if in `offer.md`), and the single next step for skimmers who jump straight to the bottom.
