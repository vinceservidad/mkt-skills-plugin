# Competitive posture playbook (Kotler)

Use Philip Kotler's four competitive postures (*Marketing Management*) to decide how a comparison or alternatives page should frame the matchup honestly, based on dated facts in `context/market.md` and `context/positioning.md`.

## Choose the right posture from evidence

| Posture | When it fits the evidence | How the comparison page frames the choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Market Leader** | Highest share or category default for the segment | Focus on expanding category clarity, total reliability, and defending core buyers without taking cheap shots at smaller rivals. |
| **Market Challenger** | Credible alternative competing directly against a larger leader | Use a **flank attack** (win an underserved use case or buyer workflow where the leader is weak) or a **bypass** (compete on a modern mechanism or model rather than fighting feature-for-feature where the leader already wins). |
| **Market Follower** | Offers similar core capability in a mature space | Never claim false novelty; compete cleanly on transparent pricing, service fit, or delivery terms backed by `offer.md`. |
| **Market Nicher** | Specializes deeply in one segment, industry, or job | Own the narrow buyer profile explicitly (*best when you are X and need Y*), and concede broader general-purpose use cases back to horizontal rivals. |

## Strict truth guardrails for every posture

- A challenger posture never allows invented competitor flaws, unverified pricing comparisons, or sneering copy.
- Concede every criterion where dated evidence shows the competitor fits better (for example broader integrations, lower starter tier, or enterprise compliance).
- State observed competitor claims with their dates (`as seen on <date>`), mark unchecked items as *not checked*, and place every competitor fact on the *Verify before publishing* checklist.
