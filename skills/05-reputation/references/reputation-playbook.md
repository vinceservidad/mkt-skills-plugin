# Reputation playbook

## The loop

1. **Listen:** reviews (wherever this business's buyers leave them), social mentions and tags, communities and forums, search results for the brand name, sales-call and support remarks. Choose sources from the evidence of where buyers actually talk.
2. **Triage:** sort each item before replying.
3. **Reply:** in the brand's voice, within a cadence the owner sets.
4. **Fix the cause:** route the operational problem to whoever owns it.
5. **Learn:** log themes and counts each period and compare.

A written loop is *designed*. It becomes *active* only when a named person or a configured tool runs it, and *verified* when someone has checked it ran. Never imply background monitoring that isn't set up.

## Triage categories

| Category | Example | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Praise | A specific compliment | Thank briefly, name the specific thing; ask permission before reusing it as a testimonial |
| Fair complaint | A real failure the records confirm | Own it, say what changes, take details private; route the cause |
| Factual error | The reviewer has a fact wrong | Correct calmly, once, without blame; take details private |
| Suspected fake or non-customer | No match in records, no detail | Neutral reply that invites contact (*we'd like to understand what happened; please reach us at …*) without saying what your records show; report through the platform's own process after checking its current policy; never accuse |
| Legal or safety issue | Data exposure, injury, discrimination claim | Escalate to the owner and legal before any public reply |
| Potential crisis | An incident spreading in public, or harm to customers | Run the crisis playbook |

## Review reply rules

- Thank, address the specific point, own what is true, say what changes, move to a private channel.
- Don't confirm in public that someone is or isn't a client, or any detail of their account or what your records show.
- Don't offer refunds, credits or discounts in public. If a guarantee may apply, the owner confirms eligibility privately.
- Don't argue, don't copy-paste the same reply, don't reward a review change.
- Replies are drafts until the owner approves them.

## Asking for reviews

Ask every customer at a natural moment (after a result, not after a complaint). No gating (asking only happy customers), no incentives conditional on a positive review, no reviews from staff or family, no fake reviews. Rules on reviews and endorsements differ by country and change; confirm the current rules with legal before launching, and check each review platform's own current policy on requests and incentives.

## Crisis communications: Situational Crisis Communication Theory (Coombs)

**Crisis clusters and responsibility:**
- **Victim:** the organisation is also a victim (natural disaster, rumour, product tampering). Low attributed responsibility.
- **Accidental:** unintentional, technical error (equipment failure, a challenge to the organisation). Moderate responsibility.
- **Preventable:** human error, a knowingly taken risk, misconduct. High responsibility.

Prior crises and a poor prior reputation raise attributed responsibility.

**Response strategies:**
- **Deny** (attack the accuser, denial, scapegoat): only when the crisis is truly a rumour or false.
- **Diminish** (excuse, justification): for accidents with minor harm; risky when people were harmed.
- **Rebuild** (apology, compensation): for preventable crises, and whenever harm is serious.
- **Bolster** (reminding of past good works, thanking stakeholders): a supplement, never the main response.

**Always first:** instructing information (what affected people should do to protect themselves) and adjusting information (concern, what we're doing to prevent a repeat).

## Crisis order of work

1. Stop the harm and preserve the records.
2. Establish confirmed facts from the business's own records; list unknowns and when each will be known.
3. Name the approver of every statement. If no crisis plan exists, set the approval chain first.
4. Flag legal and insurance review for anything involving personal or financial data, safety, employment, contracts or regulation. Notification duties (to affected people or regulators) are legal questions; this skill does not answer them.
5. Tell the directly affected parties privately, before any public statement.
6. Ask any party that received data in error to delete it and confirm (owner and legal to approve the wording).
7. Public holding statement: confirmed facts only, what we're doing, what affected people should do, when the next update comes. Avoid *one-off*, *isolated*, *fixed* or *no other data affected* until the records prove it.
8. Keep the update cadence; if timing changes, say so before the deadline passes.
9. After-action review: what failed, what was reasonable at the time, the process fix.
